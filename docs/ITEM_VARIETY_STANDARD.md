# NursePrepIQ Item Variety Standard

Every new/replacement batch must test breadth of nursing knowledge AND breadth of reasoning. Do not turn one scenario into six templated NCJMM questions.

## Required stem/task variety
For each 100-question planning block, no single task family may exceed 20%.
Target a mix of:
- assessment/finding interpretation and cue recognition
- diagnosis/condition differentiation
- medication indication, adverse effect, contraindication, interaction, monitoring
- patient teaching / teach-back / discharge understanding
- delegation, assignment, supervision, scope
- procedure/preparation/post-procedure care
- lab/diagnostic interpretation
- complication/risk recognition
- care-plan selection and modification
- expected vs unexpected finding
- evaluation of treatment/outcomes
- communication/therapeutic response
- infection prevention/safety
- calculation/dose or rate reasoning where appropriate
- prioritization/first action (important, but capped)
- NGN case-based reasoning across the six NCJMM functions

## Depth
Mix recall-supporting knowledge with application and analysis. Most items should require application/analysis rather than simple definition recall. Include multi-step reasoning and competing plausible distractors where appropriate.

## Anti-repetition rules
- Priority/first/most-important stems: <=15% of a batch.
- Same topic + same tested decision/learning objective: max 2 items per 100 unless intentionally part of a linked NGN case.
- Do not create six standalone items from the same cue paragraph merely to fill all six NCJMM functions.
- Linked NGN cases are allowed, but each item must make a distinct clinical-judgment decision and should be presented as a case set.
- Exact duplicate stems: zero.
- Near-duplicate stems or answer sets: fail review.
- Reusing a disease is acceptable only when testing a materially different competency.

## GI replacement breadth example
Coverage should span upper/lower GI, hepatobiliary and pancreatic care, nutrition/ostomy, diagnostics/procedures, medications, acute/chronic complications, teaching, pediatrics/age-specific care, RN/PN scope, safety and clinical judgment. Do not let hemorrhage, pancreatitis, or cirrhosis dominate simply because they generate easy priority scenarios.

## Answer construction
Determine the correct clinical response before answer-position balancing. Keep option text + rationale + correctness atomic. Reorder complete option objects only.
