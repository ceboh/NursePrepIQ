# NursePrepIQ NCLEX Item Alignment Standard

Version: 1.0 — September 2026

## Purpose
Every NursePrepIQ question must be an original item while being calibrated to the current official NCLEX-RN or NCLEX-PN test plan, published sample-item patterns, clinical-judgment model, and candidate-facing item formats. NursePrepIQ does not copy, reconstruct, solicit, or publish confidential/recalled NCLEX items and does not claim affiliation with NCSBN.

## Primary calibration sources
1. NCSBN 2026 NCLEX-RN Test Plan (effective Apr. 1, 2026–Mar. 31, 2029).
2. NCSBN 2026 NCLEX-PN Test Plan (effective Apr. 1, 2026–Mar. 31, 2029).
3. Official NCLEX Sample Pack and Exam Preview.
4. Official NCLEX Candidate Tutorial and NGN/Clinical Judgment resources.
5. Current authoritative clinical sources appropriate to the topic for factual validation.

The official test plans explicitly contain Client Needs definitions, nursing activity statements, content examples, sample items, clinical judgment information, and item-writing tips, and are used to guide item development/classification.

## Required metadata for every item
- Exam track: RN, PN, or genuinely shared
- Client Needs category/subcategory
- Subject/system/topic
- Nursing activity/objective
- Clinical Judgment step when applicable: Recognize Cues, Analyze Cues, Prioritize Hypotheses, Generate Solutions, Take Action, Evaluate Outcomes
- Item format
- Difficulty target
- Professional role focus
- Evidence/source note
- Validation status and immutable version

## RN/PN rule
RN and PN are not difficulty labels. They represent different entry-level roles.

RN items should appropriately emphasize comprehensive assessment and interpretation, synthesis, prioritization, planning, coordination, RN delegation/assignment, intervention, teaching, and evaluation.

PN items should appropriately emphasize focused data collection, observation and monitoring, contribution to/reinforcement of an established plan, assigned/delegated care, safety, recognition of change, and appropriate reporting/escalation.

A shared item is allowed only when both the clinical knowledge and professional decision are genuinely appropriate to both roles.

## NCLEX-style construction checklist
An item should:
1. Test an entry-level nursing decision, not trivia for its own sake.
2. Present only clinically relevant information needed to reason through the problem.
3. Prefer application/clinical judgment over simple recall when appropriate.
4. Have a clear task in the stem and avoid unintended clues to the key.
5. Use concise, professional, patient-centered language.
6. Use plausible distractors from realistic misconceptions, competing priorities, or common reasoning errors.
7. Avoid obviously absurd distractors or grammatical cues.
8. Avoid unnecessary negatives; when a negative is necessary, make it unmistakable.
9. Avoid absolute terms unless clinically justified.
10. Avoid controversial/local-scope assumptions unless the scenario establishes the setting or rule.
11. Ensure the keyed response is defensibly best based on the information supplied.
12. Ensure rationales explain clinical reasoning, not merely repeat the answer.
13. For priority questions, provide enough context for one option to outrank the others.
14. For numerical/lab/medication facts, verify current clinical accuracy before activation.
15. Never imitate an official sample so closely that the NursePrepIQ item becomes a paraphrase of protected content.

## Item-format coverage
The bank must not become four-option-only. It should progressively include original implementations of item types demonstrated in official NCLEX preparation resources, including stand-alone items and NGN clinical-judgment case-study experiences. Format selection should follow the competency being measured rather than an artificial fixed percentage, consistent with NCSBN's statement that the CAT exam does not administer a predetermined percentage of item formats.

## Clinical Judgment case studies
NGN case studies should unfold from a coherent client record and deliberately measure the clinical judgment sequence. New information must be internally consistent across the case. Each response must be scorable without relying on hidden assumptions. The student-facing experience should resemble the cognitive demands and presentation patterns demonstrated by official NCLEX resources without copying their cases.

## Validation gate
No newly generated item should move directly to trusted production status.

DRAFT -> ALIGNMENT REVIEW -> CLINICAL REVIEW -> ADVERSARIAL REVIEW -> PILOT -> METRICS REVIEW -> ACTIVE

Alignment review asks:
- Is the track correct?
- Does the decision fit RN/PN entry-level responsibility?
- Does the item map to a current test-plan activity/content area?
- Is the Client Needs classification defensible?
- Is the Clinical Judgment step correct?
- Is the cognitive demand comparable to official sample patterns?
- Is the stem focused and cue-balanced?
- Are distractors plausible and mutually distinct?
- Is there exactly one defensibly best response when the format requires one?
- Does the item avoid cueing the answer through role-focus labels or teaching text before submission?

Clinical review asks whether the scenario, key, distractors, rationale, medications, labs, thresholds, interventions, and terminology are current and safe.

Adversarial review tries to prove the item is flawed: alternate correct answer, missing assumption, ambiguous wording, role/scope conflict, outdated practice, unintended clue, poor distractor, or mismatch between stem and rationale.

## Pilot metrics
After student exposure, monitor difficulty, option selection, completion time, feedback, and discrimination when sample size permits. Poor performance triggers review rather than automatic simplification. Items with ambiguity or clinical concerns should be quarantined/reviewed.

## Existing bank
Cardiovascular and Respiratory items created before this standard remain pilot content and should be audited against this checklist. They are not automatically considered validated merely because they render and score correctly.

## Student-facing claim
Preferred wording: “Original NursePrepIQ questions aligned to the current NCLEX test plan and clinical-judgment framework.”

Do not say or imply: “actual NCLEX questions,” “official NCLEX questions,” “identical to the NCLEX,” or NCSBN endorsement/affiliation.
