### PN-A04-001 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: pediatric unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a pediatric unit is collecting data on four children. Which child should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. A 4-year-old with a fractured arm in a cast who is asking for a popsicle
- B. A 6-year-old with gastroenteritis who vomited once this morning
- **✓ C. A 2-year-old with croup who has stridor while resting quietly and new retractions**
- D. A 9-year-old with asthma who is due for a scheduled inhaler

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Stridor at rest with retractions means the child's upper airway is significantly narrowed and the child needs immediate assessment and treatment. A request for a popsicle, a single episode of vomiting, and a scheduled inhaler dose are stable situations.

### PN-A04-002 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: memory care unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a memory care unit is collecting data on four residents. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- **✓ A. A resident with new swelling, warmth, and pain in one calf**
- B. A resident who is wandering in the hallway but is easily redirected by staff members
- C. A resident who ate half of lunch
- D. A resident who is asking for a deceased spouse

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** New swelling, warmth, and pain in one calf suggest a deep vein thrombosis, which can lead to a pulmonary embolism and needs prompt evaluation. Wandering that is easily redirected, eating half a meal, and asking for a deceased spouse are common in dementia and do not signal an acute change.

### PN-A04-003 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: newborn nursery

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in a newborn nursery is collecting data on four newborns. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. Bluish hands and feet in a 3-hour-old newborn whose lips and trunk are pink and warm
- **✓ B. Yellow skin on the face and chest of a newborn who is 16 hours old**
- C. Small white spots on the nose of a newborn who is 1 day old and feeding well
- D. A heart rate of 140/min in a newborn

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Jaundice that appears within the first 24 hours of life is pathologic, often from hemolysis, and needs prompt evaluation. Acrocyanosis with a pink trunk, milia, and a heart rate within the normal range of 110 to 160/min are expected newborn findings.

### PN-A04-004 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: oncology unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on an oncology unit is collecting data on four clients. Which client should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. A client receiving radiation to the chest who reports feeling more tired than usual this week
- B. A client with breast cancer who asks for a wig catalog before chemotherapy starts next week
- C. A client with prostate cancer who wants to talk with the dietitian about meal choices
- **✓ D. A client with lung cancer who has new back pain and leg weakness**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** New back pain with leg weakness in a client with cancer suggests spinal cord compression from metastasis, an emergency that can cause permanent paralysis without rapid treatment. Fatigue during radiation is expected, and requests for a wig catalog or a dietitian are routine.

### PN-A04-005 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: medical unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a medical unit receives report on four clients. Which client should the LPN/VN check on first?

- B. A client with diabetes who is asking when breakfast will arrive
- C. A client with cellulitis whose redness is within the marked border
- **✓ D. A client with cirrhosis who just vomited a large amount of bright red blood**

**Answer:** D

### PN-A04-006 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Timing of medication documentation

**Stem:** To save time, an LPN/VN documents morning medications as given before taking them to the residents. How should this practice be described?

- **✓ A. Unacceptable, because documentation must reflect care that has actually been given**
- B. Acceptable, as long as the LPN/VN gives each of the medications within the next hour
- C. Acceptable on busy days
- D. Unacceptable only if a resident later refuses a dose

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Documenting care before it is given creates a false record and can lead to errors, such as another nurse assuming a dose was given or a refusal going unrecorded. Medications are documented after they are given. Being busy does not make the practice acceptable, and it is wrong whether or not a resident refuses.

### PN-A04-007 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Responding to a privacy breach

**Stem:** The LPN/VN realizes that an email containing a client's lab results was sent to the wrong outside address. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Send a follow-up email asking the person to ignore the message and say nothing else
- B. Wait to see whether the person who received the email responds to it
- **✓ C. Report it right away to the supervisor or privacy officer**
- D. Delete the sent email

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Sending protected health information to the wrong recipient is a breach that must be reported promptly so the facility can take steps to contain it and meet legal notification requirements. Handling it privately, waiting, or deleting the sent message hides the breach and does not protect the client.

### PN-A04-008 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Tasks the LPN/VN has not been trained to do

**Stem:** The charge nurse asks the LPN/VN to access a client's implanted port. The LPN/VN has never been trained or checked off on this skill. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Access the port anyway, since the charge nurse is responsible for the assignment
- **✓ B. Tell the charge nurse they have not been trained in this skill**
- C. Watch a video about implanted ports and then access the port without supervision
- D. Ask the assistive personnel to help, since two people make the procedure safer

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Nurses are accountable for performing only tasks they are competent and authorized to do. The LPN/VN tells the charge nurse so the task can be reassigned or done with proper training and validation per facility policy and state scope. An assignment does not transfer accountability, a video does not establish competence, and an AP cannot assist in a way that makes the task safe.

### PN-A04-009 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Practicing with an expired license

**Stem:** Before a shift, the LPN/VN realizes their nursing license expired 2 days ago because they forgot to renew it. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Work the shift and renew the license online at the end of the shift
- **✓ B. Tell the supervisor and not work as a nurse until the license is renewed**
- C. Work only with stable clients until the license is renewed
- D. Ask another nurse to sign the documentation

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Practicing nursing with an expired license is practicing without a license, which is illegal regardless of the client assignment. The LPN/VN informs the supervisor and does not work as a nurse until the license is active. Having another nurse sign for care falsifies the record.

### PN-A04-010 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Addressing a coworker's unsafe practice

**Stem:** The LPN/VN notices that a coworker repeatedly goes from one resident to another without performing hand hygiene. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Ignore it, since the coworker has more experience on the unit than the LPN/VN has
- B. Tell the residents to watch the coworker
- C. Post a complaint on social media
- **✓ D. Remind the coworker directly and, if it continues, report it per policy**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Skipped hand hygiene spreads infection. A direct, respectful reminder addresses the problem in the moment, and repeated lapses are reported through the facility's process. Experience does not excuse unsafe practice, involving residents is inappropriate, and social media posts breach professional standards without solving the problem.

### PN-A04-011 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Fatigue and safe practice

**Stem:** At the end of a 12-hour shift, the supervisor asks the LPN/VN to stay for another 8 hours. The LPN/VN feels too tired to work safely. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Decline the extra shift and explain the safety concern to the supervisor**
- B. Accept the extra shift and drink several cups of coffee to stay alert all night
- C. Accept the extra shift but ask to care for only the most stable clients on the unit
- D. Leave without giving report

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Nurses are accountable for being fit to practice, and severe fatigue increases the risk of errors. Declining the extra shift and explaining why is appropriate, and the LPN/VN still gives a full report before leaving. Caffeine does not reverse fatigue-related impairment, and leaving without report can be abandonment.

### PN-A04-014 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

### PN-A04-025 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Interpreters for clients who are deaf

**Stem:** A client who is deaf and uses American Sign Language is being admitted. Which action by the LPN/VN is most appropriate?

- A. Ask the client's teenage child to interpret during the admission interview
- B. Rely on written notes for all communication
- **✓ C. Arrange for a qualified sign language interpreter for important discussions**
- D. Speak loudly and slowly

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Clients who are deaf are entitled to effective communication, which for complex discussions such as admission, consent, and teaching usually means a qualified sign language interpreter, in person or by video, at no cost to the client. Family members, especially children, should not interpret. Written notes may help for simple needs, but English may not be the client's first language, and speaking loudly does not help.

**Answer:** Condition: Family conflict with the client's valid DNR order. Actions: Follow the DNR order and notify the RN and provider of the son's request; Listen to the son and offer support, such as a chaplain or social worker. Monitor: Signs of pain or distress; Breathing pattern and comfort.
