## PN-C01: Urinary tract infection with acute confusion in a long-term care resident

System: Renal & Urinary · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** An 84-year-old resident of a long-term care facility has mild Alzheimer's disease and is usually oriented to person and place. This morning, the assistive personnel tells the LPN/VN that the resident is "not acting like usual."

**Nurses' notes, 0700:** Confused to place and time, which is new. Restless and picking at bed linens. Attention comes and goes during conversation. Incontinent of cloudy, foul-smelling urine twice overnight and says urinating "burns." Drank about 400 mL of fluid yesterday. Oriented to person. Lungs clear. Skin intact.

**Vital signs, 0700:** T 100.9°F (38.3°C), HR 104/min, RR 20/min, BP 118/70 mm Hg (usual 136/78), SpO2 96% on room air.

**Care plan baseline:** Oriented to person and place, with short-term memory loss for the past 2 years. Continent with reminders every 2 hours. Walks with a walker. Usually drinks about 1,200 mL a day.

## PN-C02: Cellulitis of the lower leg in a client with diabetes

System: Integumentary · Discipline: Adult Health

## PN-C03: Digoxin toxicity in an older adult

System: Cardiovascular · Discipline: Pharmacology

**Scenario:** An 81-year-old resident of a long-term care facility with heart failure takes digoxin and furosemide. For 2 days, the resident has had nausea and a poor appetite.

**Nurses' notes, 0800:** Nauseated for 2 days and ate 25% of meals. States that lights look like they have yellow-green halos. More tired than usual. Apical pulse 52/min and irregular, counted for 1 full minute. Fine crackles at both lung bases, unchanged from last week. Refused oral potassium for the past 3 days because of nausea.

**Vital signs, 0800:** T 98.2°F (36.8°C), HR 52/min apical, RR 18/min, BP 108/64 mm Hg.

**Laboratory results, 0600:** Potassium 3.1 mEq/L (3.5–5.0), digoxin 2.8 ng/mL (0.5–2.0), creatinine 1.8 mg/dL (0.6–1.2), BUN 30 mg/dL (7–20).

**Medication administration record:** Digoxin 0.25 mg by mouth daily at 0900. Furosemide 40 mg by mouth daily at 0900. Potassium chloride 20 mEq by mouth daily at 0900.

### PN-C03-01 · Highlight · Pharmacological Therapies · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that require follow-up.

Key (highlight): Nauseated for 2 days; Yellow-green halos around lights; Apical pulse 52/min and irregular; Refused oral potassium for 3 days; Potassium 3.1 mEq/L; Digoxin 2.8 ng/mL; Creatinine 1.8 mg/dL

- s1: Nauseated for 2 days
- s2: Yellow-green halos around lights
- s3: Apical pulse 52/min and irregular
- s4: Fine crackles at both bases, unchanged
- s5: Refused oral potassium for 3 days
- s6: Potassium 3.1 mEq/L
- s7: Digoxin 2.8 ng/mL
- s8: Creatinine 1.8 mg/dL

**Rationale:** Nausea, visual halos, and a slow, irregular pulse are signs of digoxin toxicity, which is confirmed by a level above the therapeutic range. Low potassium, missed potassium doses, and reduced kidney function raise the risk. Unchanged crackles and a normal temperature are not new concerns.

## PN-C04: Toddler with a febrile seizure

System: Neurologic · Discipline: Pediatrics

**Scenario:** An 18-month-old is brought to an urgent care clinic 20 minutes after a seizure at home. The toddler has had a runny nose since yesterday and a fever that began this morning.

**Parent report:** The whole body stiffened and then jerked for about 2 minutes, then stopped on its own. No previous seizures. Immunizations are up to date. No recent falls or injuries.

**Nurses' notes:** Drowsy but opens eyes to voice and cries when examined. Moves all extremities. Neck supple. Clear nasal drainage. No rash.

**Vital signs:** T 103.1°F (39.5°C) tympanic, HR 150/min, RR 32/min, SpO2 98% on room air. Weight 11 kg.

### PN-C04-01 · Highlight · Physiological Adaptation · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that meet the criteria for a simple febrile seizure.

Key (highlight): Age 18 months; Whole body stiffened and then jerked; Lasted about 2 minutes; Stopped on its own; Fever that began this morning

- s1: Age 18 months
- s2: Whole body stiffened and then jerked
- s3: Lasted about 2 minutes
- s4: Stopped on its own
- s5: Fever that began this morning
- s6: Clear nasal drainage
- s7: Immunizations are up to date
- s8: SpO2 98% on room air

**Answer:** Age 18 months; Whole body stiffened and then jerked; Lasted about 2 minutes; Stopped on its own; Fever that began this morning

**Rationale:** A simple febrile seizure occurs in a child between 6 months and 5 years old with a fever, is generalized, lasts less than 15 minutes, stops on its own, and happens once in 24 hours. Nasal drainage, immunization status, and oxygen saturation are not part of the definition.

## PN-C05: Major depressive disorder with suicidal thoughts
