### PN-A03-001 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: clinic phone messages

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in a family practice clinic is reviewing phone messages to route to the RN and provider. Which message needs the quickest follow-up?

- A. A client asks whether the clinic can send a refill of a blood pressure medicine
- B. A parent asks whether a 4-year-old can return to preschool after a cold
- C. A client wants to move a routine physical to a different week
- **✓ D. A parent reports that their 6-week-old infant has a temperature of 100.6°F (38.1°C)**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher in an infant younger than 3 months can signal a serious bacterial infection and needs same-day, urgent evaluation. A refill request, a question about returning to preschool after a cold, and rescheduling a physical are routine.

### PN-A03-002 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: surgical unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a surgical unit is collecting data on four clients. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN immediately?

- A. Incisional pain of 3/10 that the client rates as tolerable 1 day after a laparoscopic appendectomy
- **✓ B. Sudden shortness of breath and chest pain 3 days after a hip replacement**
- C. Mild nausea after the first clear liquid meal
- D. A request for help to the bathroom

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Sudden dyspnea and chest pain after orthopedic surgery suggest a pulmonary embolism, a life-threatening emergency. Tolerable pain, mild nausea when starting a diet, and a request for help to the bathroom can be addressed after the emergency is reported.

### PN-A03-003 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Physiological needs first

**Stem:** The LPN/VN has just received report. Which client should the LPN/VN see first?

- **✓ A. A client with diabetes who is shaky, sweaty, and confused before lunch**
- B. A client who is crying after learning that discharge will be delayed by a day
- C. A client who wants help filling out a menu for tomorrow's meals and snacks
- D. A client who wants a shower

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Shakiness, sweating, and confusion in a client with diabetes suggest hypoglycemia, a physiological problem that can quickly worsen and must be treated first. Emotional distress about a delay, menu selection, and a shower are important but can wait briefly.

### PN-A03-004 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: dialysis unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in an outpatient dialysis unit is collecting data on four clients. Which client should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. A client who reports feeling tired after the last three dialysis sessions
- B. A client who asks for a blanket because the treatment area feels cold today
- **✓ C. A client who develops chest pain during dialysis**
- D. A client who asks when the dietitian will be available to talk about meal plans

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Chest pain during dialysis may signal myocardial ischemia from hypotension or fluid shifts, or another emergency, and needs immediate RN assessment. Fatigue after dialysis is common, and requests for a blanket or the dietitian are routine.

### PN-A03-005 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Resident rights: communication

**Stem:** The family of a competent resident in long-term care asks the LPN/VN to stop putting through the resident's phone calls from a friend the family dislikes. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Explain that the resident has the right to communicate with people they choose**
- B. Block the friend's calls, since the family knows what is best
- C. Ask the friend to stop calling the facility without telling the resident
- D. Let the facility's receptionist decide whether to put the calls through

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Residents in long-term care have the right to private communication with people of their choosing, including by phone. A competent resident's family cannot restrict that right, and staff should not secretly block calls or leave the decision to others. The LPN/VN can involve the RN or social worker if the family has safety concerns.

### PN-A03-006 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Request to self-administer medications

**Stem:** A resident in long-term care asks to keep their own inhaler at the bedside and use it without staff help. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Tell the resident that all medications in long-term care must be given by staff, with no exceptions
- B. Give the inhaler to the resident to keep
- **✓ C. Report the request to the RN so the team can assess whether self-administration is safe**
- D. Refuse and document the request

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Residents have the right to self-administer medications if the interdisciplinary team determines that it is clinically appropriate and safe. The LPN/VN reports the request so the assessment can be done. A blanket refusal ignores the resident's rights, and handing over the inhaler without an assessment and a prescription bypasses the process.

### PN-A03-008 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Consent for a minor's surgery

**Stem:** A 15-year-old who is not emancipated is scheduled for an elective knee repair. Who should sign the surgical consent?

- A. The 15-year-old, since the surgery is being done on the teen's own body
- B. The surgeon, since the procedure is elective and was recommended by the surgeon
- C. Any adult relative who comes to the hospital with the teen on the day of surgery
- **✓ D. The parent or legal guardian**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** For most procedures, a minor who is not emancipated needs consent from a parent or legal guardian, although the teen should be included in the discussion and asked to agree. A surgeon cannot consent for the client, and other relatives cannot consent unless they are the legal guardian.

### PN-A03-009 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Implied consent in an emergency

**Stem:** An unconscious adult with internal bleeding after a car crash needs emergency surgery, and no family can be reached. Which statement about consent is accurate?

- **✓ A. Consent is implied, so the surgery can proceed to preserve the client's life and health**
- B. The surgery must wait until a family member can be found to sign the consent form
- C. The LPN/VN can sign the consent form for the client, since the client is unconscious
- D. A court order is required before any emergency surgery can begin

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** In a true emergency, when a client cannot consent and no surrogate is available, consent is implied for treatment needed to prevent death or serious harm. Waiting for family or a court order could cost the client's life, and nurses do not sign consent on a client's behalf.

### PN-A03-010 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Living will brought on admission

**Stem:** A client being admitted hands the LPN/VN a copy of their living will. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Return it to the client, since living wills apply only when a client is in a nursing home
- B. Ask the client to sign a new living will on the facility's own form
- **✓ C. Make sure a copy goes into the record and tell the RN so the provider is aware**
- D. Give it to the family to keep

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** An advance directive brought on admission is placed in the record and communicated to the RN and provider so the client's wishes guide care. Living wills apply in any care setting, a valid directive does not need to be redone on a facility form, and giving it to the family keeps it from the team.

### PN-A03-011 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Request to witness a will

**Stem:** A resident asks the LPN/VN to sign as a witness on the resident's new will for their property. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Sign right away, since witnessing a will is one of the LPN/VN's legal duties
- **✓ B. Follow facility policy, which usually refers the request to social services**
- C. Ask an assistive personnel on the unit to sign as the witness instead of the nurse
- D. Read the will and advise the resident

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Witnessing a legal document such as a property will is not part of nursing practice, and many facilities prohibit staff from doing it because of possible conflicts of interest. The LPN/VN follows policy, which often directs these requests to social services or the resident's attorney. Asking an AP to sign does not solve the problem, and nurses do not give legal advice.

### PN-A03-012 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Photographing a wound

**Stem:** The wound care protocol calls for weekly photographs of a resident's pressure injury. Which action by the LPN/VN is correct?

- A. Take the photo with a personal phone and delete it after uploading it to the record
- B. Text the photo to the wound nurse from a personal phone so the nurse sees it sooner
- C. Post the photo in the unit's group chat so all staff can follow how the wound is healing
- **✓ D. Use the facility's approved device and follow its photo policy**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Client photographs are protected health information. They are taken with facility-approved devices, with consent as policy requires, and stored only in the record. Personal phones and texting can expose images even after they are deleted, and group chats share them with people who do not need them.

### PN-A03-013 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Questions about herbal supplements

**Stem:** A client taking several prescribed medications asks the LPN/VN whether it is safe to start taking St. John's wort. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Tell the client that herbal supplements are natural and are safe with any medication
- **✓ B. Advise checking with the pharmacist or provider, since herbs can interact with medications**
- C. Tell the client to start the supplement and stop it if any side effects develop
- D. Suggest that the client take a double dose to see results faster

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Herbal supplements can interact with prescribed drugs; St. John's wort, for example, reduces the effect of many medications and can cause serotonin syndrome with some antidepressants. The LPN/VN refers the question to the pharmacist or provider. Natural products are not automatically safe, and starting or increasing a supplement without review can cause harm.

### PN-A03-015 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Correcting an AP's unsafe practice

**Stem:** The LPN/VN sees an assistive personnel (AP) walking an unsteady resident without a gait belt. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Wait until the walk is finished and then remind the AP about gait belts at the end of the shift

- **✓ C. Help steady the resident and have the AP apply a gait belt**

### PN-A03-016 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Reviewing the need for a urinary catheter

**Stem:** A client has had an indwelling urinary catheter since surgery 4 days ago, is now walking to the bathroom with help, and has no documented reason for the catheter. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Ask the RN whether the catheter is still needed**
- B. Leave the catheter in place until the client is discharged, since it is more convenient for staff
- C. Remove the catheter without a prescription because it is no longer needed
- D. Clamp the catheter for several hours to retrain the bladder before discharge

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** The risk of catheter-associated urinary tract infection rises each day a catheter stays in, so evidence-based practice calls for daily review and prompt removal when it is no longer indicated. The LPN/VN raises the question with the RN so the provider or protocol can authorize removal. Convenience is not an indication, removal requires a prescription or protocol, and clamping is not recommended routinely.

### PN-A03-017 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Finding client information in a public area

**Stem:** The LPN/VN finds another nurse's printed report sheet with client names and diagnoses on a table in the hospital cafeteria. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Leave the sheet on the table, since the nurse who wrote it will probably come back for it
- B. Throw the sheet in the cafeteria trash can so that no one else picks it up
- C. Read the sheet to find out which unit it came from and return it later
- **✓ D. Secure the sheet and return it to the unit or dispose of it per policy, and report the breach**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Client information left in a public place is a privacy breach. The LPN/VN secures the sheet right away, returns it or shreds it according to policy, and reports the incident so the facility can respond as required. Leaving it exposes the information, regular trash is not secure, and reading more than needed adds to the breach.

### PN-A03-018 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Facility directory and visitor requests

**Stem:** A visitor asks the LPN/VN for a client's room number. The client asked on admission not to be listed in the facility directory. What should the LPN/VN say?

### PN-A03-019 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Family request to withhold a diagnosis

**Stem:** The family of a competent client asks the LPN/VN not to tell the client about a new diagnosis of cancer. What should the LPN/VN do?

- B. Agree to keep the diagnosis from the client, since the family knows the client best of all
- C. Tell the client the diagnosis immediately, before the provider can discuss it with them

**Answer:** A

### PN-A03-023 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Reporting a critical lab value

**Stem:** The laboratory calls the LPN/VN with a critical potassium value of 6.8 mEq/L for a client. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Write the result on a sticky note and give it to the RN at the next break
- B. Wait for the result to appear in the record before taking action
- **✓ C. Read the result back and notify the RN and provider immediately**
- D. Recheck the potassium level

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Critical results must be communicated to the responsible provider promptly. The nurse who receives the call writes down and reads back the result to confirm it and then reports it right away. A potassium level of 6.8 mEq/L can cause fatal dysrhythmias, so delays are dangerous, and the LPN/VN cannot order a new test.

### PN-A03-024 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Reporting a missed finding

**Stem:** Near the end of the shift, the LPN/VN realizes they forgot to report a resident's new ankle swelling noticed that morning. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Leave the finding out of the report, since the next shift will notice the swelling on its own
- **✓ B. Report it to the RN now and document it accurately**
- C. Document the swelling with the morning time and say it was reported then
- D. Wait until tomorrow to see whether the swelling goes away on its own

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** The nurse is accountable for reporting findings. Reporting late is better than not reporting, so the LPN/VN reports now and documents accurately with the actual time. Assuming others will notice, back-timing an entry to suggest it was reported earlier, and waiting are all unsafe or dishonest.

### PN-A03-025 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Supporting a client's decision

**Stem:** A client who must choose between surgery and radiation therapy asks the LPN/VN, "What would you do if you were me?" What is the LPN/VN's best response?

- A. "If I were you, I would choose the surgery, since it is the faster way to be finished with this."
- B. "You should do whatever your family thinks is best for you."
- C. "I'm not allowed to talk about it."
- **✓ D. "What matters most to you as you think about each choice? I can let your provider know you have questions."**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** The nurse supports the client's decision-making by helping the client explore their own values and priorities and by making sure the provider answers remaining questions. Giving a personal recommendation can unduly influence the client, deferring to the family does not respect the client's autonomy, and refusing to talk leaves the client without support.

### PN-A03-026 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Preoperative checklist

**Stem:** While completing a preoperative checklist, the LPN/VN finds that the surgical consent form has not been signed. The transport staff has arrived. What should the LPN/VN do?

- B. Send the client to surgery and tell the surgeon to obtain the consent in the preoperative holding area

Sending the client without it risks proceeding without valid consent, and a family member cannot sign for a competent adult.

### PN-A03-027 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Rationale:** The provider's call concerns a possibly unstable resident and allows treatment decisions to be made, so it comes first. A request for water can be handled by an AP or right after the call, and the visitor can be asked to wait briefly. A visitor cannot take a clinical call.
