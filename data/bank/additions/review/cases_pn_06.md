## PN-C26: Surgical site infection after a total knee replacement

System: Musculoskeletal · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** The home health LPN/VN visits a 67-year-old client with type 2 diabetes who had a right total knee replacement 9 days ago.

**Nurses' notes:** Incision red and warm for 2 cm on each side, with thick yellow drainage from the lower end. Knee more swollen than at the last visit. Pain 7/10, up from 3/10 two days ago. Has stopped doing range-of-motion exercises because of pain. Left calf soft and nontender. Home glucose readings 210 to 260 mg/dL this week.

**Vital signs:** T 100.9°F (38.3°C), HR 102/min, RR 18/min, BP 128/76 mm Hg.

**Medications:** Aspirin 81 mg by mouth twice daily for clot prevention. Metformin 1,000 mg by mouth twice daily. Oxycodone 5 mg by mouth every 4 hours as needed for pain.

## PN-C27: Influenza outbreak in long-term care

System: Respiratory · Discipline: Safety & Infection Control

**Scenario:** In January, three residents on one hall of a long-term care facility develop fever and cough within 48 hours. The LPN/VN is the charge nurse on the evening shift.

**Nurses' notes, 1600:** Resident A, 84 years old: T 101.6°F (38.7°C), cough, and body aches since this morning; rapid influenza test positive. Resident B, 79 years old: T 100.8°F (38.2°C), new cough, and sore throat since yesterday. Resident C, 91 years old: T 102.2°F (39°C), RR 28/min, SpO2 89% on room air, and new confusion.

**Vaccination records:** All three residents received the influenza vaccine in October. Two of the evening staff members declined the influenza vaccine this season.

**Facility policy:** Use droplet precautions for suspected or confirmed influenza. Report suspected outbreaks to the RN supervisor and the infection preventionist. The provider decides on antiviral treatment and prophylaxis.

- s3: Resident C: RR 28/min and SpO2 89% on room air

**Answer:** Three residents with fever and cough within 48 hours; Rapid influenza test positive; Resident C: RR 28/min and SpO2 89% on room air; Resident C: new confusion; Two evening staff declined the influenza vaccine

**Rationale:** A cluster of respiratory illness with a positive influenza test suggests an outbreak, Resident C has signs of severe illness, and unvaccinated staff increase the risk of spread. ## PN-C28: Hyperemesis gravidarum

System: Gastrointestinal · Discipline: Maternal & Newborn

**Scenario:** A client at 10 weeks of gestation is admitted with nausea and vomiting that have prevented eating or drinking for 2 days.

**Nurses' notes:** Vomiting 8 to 10 times a day for a week. Unable to keep fluids down for 24 hours. Weight 121 lb (54.9 kg), down from 130 lb (59 kg) before pregnancy. Mucous membranes dry. Dizzy when standing. Says, "I can't keep living like this. I'm afraid I'm hurting my baby."

**Vital signs:** T 98.9°F (37.2°C), HR 112/min, RR 18/min, BP 98/60 mm Hg sitting and 86/52 mm Hg standing.

**Laboratory results:** Urine ketones 3+ (negative). Potassium 3.2 mEq/L (3.5–5.0). Urine specific gravity 1.032 (1.005–1.030).

**Prescriptions:** IV fluids with potassium. Thiamine 100 mg IV daily. Ondansetron 4 mg IV every 8 hours as needed. NPO until vomiting is controlled, then small, frequent bland meals.

## PN-C29: Vaso-occlusive pain crisis in a school-age child with sickle cell disease

### PN-C30-04 · Select all that apply · Coordinated Care · Generate solutions

### PN-C30-05 · Multiple choice · Pharmacological Therapies · Take action

**Topic:** Case study: Take action

**Stem:** The COWS score is 14, and buprenorphine 4 mg sublingual is due. Which instruction should the LPN/VN give the client?

- **✓ A. Let the tablet dissolve completely under the tongue without chewing or swallowing it**
- B. Swallow the tablet whole with a full glass of water so that it is absorbed faster in the stomach
- C. Chew the tablet thoroughly and then swallow it with juice so that it starts working sooner
- D. Dissolve it in coffee

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Sublingual buprenorphine is absorbed through the oral mucosa and is much less effective if swallowed, so the client lets it dissolve fully under the tongue and avoids eating or drinking until it has dissolved.
