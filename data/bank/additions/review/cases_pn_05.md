## PN-C21: Atelectasis after abdominal surgery

System: Respiratory · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** A 54-year-old client is on the second day after an open colectomy. The client smokes 1 pack of cigarettes a day and is reluctant to move because of pain.

**Nurses' notes, 0900:** Splints the abdomen and takes shallow breaths. Diminished breath sounds with fine crackles at both bases. Weak, nonproductive cough. Has used the incentive spirometer twice since last evening, reaching 500 mL (goal 1,500 mL). Has stayed in bed since surgery. Pain 7/10; the last oxycodone dose was 6 hours ago.

**Vital signs, 0900:** T 100.2°F (37.9°C), HR 98/min, RR 24/min and shallow, BP 132/78 mm Hg, SpO2 91% on room air.

**Prescriptions:** Oxycodone 5 mg by mouth every 4 hours as needed for pain. Incentive spirometer 10 times an hour while awake. Walk in the hallway 3 times a day. Oxygen by nasal cannula to keep SpO2 at 92% or higher.

## PN-C22: Herpes zoster in an older adult

System: Integumentary · Discipline: Safety & Infection Control

**Scenario:** A 74-year-old resident of an assisted living facility has had burning pain on the right side of the chest for 3 days. Today, a rash has appeared in the same area.

**Nurses' notes:** Clusters of fluid-filled blisters on red skin in a band from the spine to the front of the right chest, stopping at the midline. Burning pain 8/10, worse when clothing touches the area. The lesions have not crusted. The resident's roommate is receiving chemotherapy. One staff member on the unit is pregnant and has never had chickenpox.

**Vital signs:** T 99.4°F (37.4°C), HR 88/min, RR 16/min, BP 138/80 mm Hg.

**History:** Had chickenpox as a child. Has not received the shingles vaccine.

### PN-C22-01 · Highlight · Physiological Adaptation · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that require follow-up.

Key (highlight): Fluid-filled blisters in a band on the right chest; Burning pain 8/10; The lesions have not crusted; Roommate is receiving chemotherapy; Pregnant staff member who has never had chickenpox

- s1: Fluid-filled blisters in a band on the right chest
- s2: Burning pain 8/10
- s3: The lesions have not crusted
- s4: Roommate is receiving chemotherapy
- s5: Pregnant staff member who has never had chickenpox
- s6: T 99.4°F (37.4°C)
- s7: RR 16/min

**Answer:** Fluid-filled blisters in a band on the right chest; Burning pain 8/10; The lesions have not crusted; Roommate is receiving chemotherapy; Pregnant staff member who has never had chickenpox

**Rationale:** A painful vesicular rash in a band on one side of the body suggests shingles, and uncrusted lesions can spread varicella-zoster virus to people who are not immune, such as an immunocompromised roommate or a pregnant staff member without immunity. The temperature and respiratory rate are not concerns.

### PN-C22-04 · Select all that apply · Safety and Infection Control · Generate solutions

**Topic:** Case study: Generate solutions

**Stem:** The resident is not immunocompromised, and the rash is localized. Which infection control measures should the LPN/VN put in place? Select all that apply.

- **✓ D. Keep staff without immunity from caring for the resident**
- E. Allow the pregnant staff member to give care while wearing gloves

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** For localized shingles in a person who is not immunocompromised, standard precautions with the lesions completely covered until they crust are used, along with hand hygiene and separating the resident from people at high risk. Gloves do not protect a nonimmune pregnant staff member, and precautions are based on the lesions crusting, not on pain. Disseminated shingles or shingles in an immunocompromised person requires airborne and contact precautions.

## PN-C24: Nephrotic syndrome in a preschool child

### PN-C25-05 · Multiple choice · Pharmacological Therapies · Take action
