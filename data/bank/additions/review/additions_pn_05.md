### PN-A05-001 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: urgent care clinic

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in an urgent care clinic is checking in four clients. Which client should the provider see first?

- A. A 25-year-old with an ankle injury from basketball who can bear weight on it
- B. A 40-year-old with burning and urgency on urination since yesterday
- **✓ C. A 64-year-old whose speech became slurred and whose face began drooping 30 minutes ago**
- D. A 52-year-old who needs a tetanus booster after a minor cut

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Sudden slurred speech and facial drooping are signs of stroke, and treatment depends on time since onset, so the provider is notified at once and emergency services activated per protocol. An ankle injury with weight bearing, urinary symptoms, and a tetanus booster can wait.

### PN-A05-002 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: endoscopy recovery

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in an endoscopy recovery area is collecting data on four clients. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN immediately?

- A. Mild bloating and passing gas after a colonoscopy, with stable vital signs and no pain at all
- **✓ B. New chest pain and crackling under the skin of the neck after an upper endoscopy**
- C. A sore throat after an upper endoscopy
- D. Drowsiness that is improving

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Chest pain with crackling under the skin (subcutaneous emphysema) after an upper endoscopy suggests esophageal perforation, an emergency. Bloating from insufflated air, a mild sore throat, and improving drowsiness after sedation are expected.

### PN-A05-003 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Referral for alcohol use treatment

**Stem:** A client admitted with alcohol-related pancreatitis tells the LPN/VN, "I want to stop drinking, but I don't know where to start." What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Tell the client to wait until after discharge to think about stopping drinking
- B. Suggest that the client cut back slowly on their own without telling the provider
- C. Change the subject
- **✓ D. Tell the RN so the team can arrange a referral to a treatment program**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A client who expresses readiness to change should be connected with help, such as a treatment program, counseling, or mutual-help groups, and the provider needs to know because stopping alcohol can cause dangerous withdrawal. Delaying the conversation misses the opportunity, cutting back without medical guidance can be unsafe, and changing the subject dismisses the client.

### PN-A05-004 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

### PN-A05-005 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Discharge after moderate sedation

**Stem:** A client who received moderate sedation for an outpatient procedure tells the LPN/VN, "I'll just take a taxi home by myself." What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Explain that a responsible adult must go with the client, and tell the RN**
- B. Let the client leave once they can walk to the elevator without any help
- C. Agree, since a taxi is safer than driving after the sedation
- D. Ask the taxi driver to stay with the client overnight

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** After sedation, judgment and reaction time can be impaired for hours, so facilities require a responsible adult to accompany the client home. The LPN/VN explains this and tells the RN so the discharge can be arranged safely. Walking unassisted does not show that sedation has worn off, a taxi alone does not provide supervision, and a driver cannot be responsible for the client.

### PN-A05-006 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

- B. Tell the AP to try again tomorrow
- C. Order a supplement
- **✓ D. Check with the resident to find out why, such as pain, nausea, or a dental problem**

### PN-A05-007 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Assigning residents with infection risks

**Stem:** The LPN/VN is assigning residents to assistive personnel (AP). Which pair of residents should the LPN/VN avoid assigning to the same AP?

- A. A resident who needs help with meals and a resident who walks with a walker in the hallway
- B. A resident with dementia who wanders and a resident with a hearing impairment who needs cueing
- **✓ C. A resident with C. difficile diarrhea and a resident receiving chemotherapy**
- D. Two residents who are independent

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** When possible, the same caregiver should not care for a resident with a transmissible infection and a resident whose immune system is suppressed, to reduce the risk of spreading infection. The other pairings do not create this risk.

### PN-A05-009 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Escalating an emergency in long-term care

**Stem:** At night in a long-term care facility, a resident suddenly develops slurred speech and weakness of one arm. The RN supervisor cannot be reached. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Wait until the RN supervisor can be reached before taking any other action for the resident
- B. Put the resident to bed and recheck the symptoms in the morning
- **✓ C. Follow the facility's emergency protocol, such as calling 911, and keep trying to reach the RN**
- D. Give the resident aspirin from the stock supply

**Answer:** C

### PN-A05-012 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Stem:** A competent resident with dysphagia was taught the risks of thin liquids by the speech-language pathologist and still chooses to drink regular coffee. What should the LPN/VN do?

- C. Remove all coffee from the resident's meal trays until the resident agrees to drink thickened liquids

**Answer:** B

### PN-A05-019 · Bow-tie · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Postpartum psychosis

**Stem:** On the third day after giving birth, a client tells the LPN/VN that voices are saying the baby is evil. The client has not slept for 2 nights, is pacing the room, and is talking rapidly. - Actions to take (choose 2): Stay with the client and do not leave the client alone with the infant / Report the findings to the RN and provider immediately / Reassure the client that these feelings are common after birth / Encourage the client to keep the infant in the room overnight / Suggest a nap and recheck the client tomorrow

### PN-A05-021 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Young children's understanding of death

- A. "Grandpa is sleeping now, and he will stay asleep for a very, very long time, so you won't see him for a while."

### PN-A05-023 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Suicide risk early in antidepressant treatment

**Stem:** A client who started an antidepressant 2 weeks ago now has more energy but still feels hopeless. Why should the LPN/VN monitor the client closely?

- A. The medication will cause a dangerous rise in blood pressure within days
- **✓ B. Suicide risk may rise as energy returns**
- C. Increased energy means the medication should be stopped right away
- D. Hopelessness is a common side effect of antidepressants that requires a dose increase

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Antidepressants often improve energy before mood, so a client who still feels hopeless may now have the energy to act on suicidal thoughts. The risk is highest early in treatment and after dose changes, especially in young people. Increased energy is not a reason to stop the drug, and hopelessness is a symptom of depression, not a side effect.

### PN-A05-024 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Splitting in borderline personality disorder

**Stem:** A client with borderline personality disorder tells the LPN/VN, "You're the only nurse who understands me. The others are terrible." What is the LPN/VN's best response?

### PN-A05-030 · Multiple choice · Physiological Adaptation

**Topic:** Clear nasal drainage after a head injury

**Stem:** A client who fell and struck their head has clear, watery drainage from the nose. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- **✓ A. Report it to the RN immediately, since it may be cerebrospinal fluid from a skull fracture**
- B. Have the client blow the nose gently to clear the drainage before checking it again
- C. Pack the nose with gauze to stop the drainage
- D. Suction the nose

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Clear drainage from the nose after a head injury may be cerebrospinal fluid from a basilar skull fracture, which creates a risk of meningitis. The LPN/VN reports it immediately and lets it drain freely onto a loose pad. Blowing the nose, packing it, or nasal suctioning can increase pressure, introduce bacteria, or push material through the fracture.

### PN-A05-031 · Multiple choice · Physiological Adaptation

**Topic:** Choking in an adult

**Stem:** A resident who is eating suddenly grasps the throat, cannot speak or cough, and is turning blue. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Give the resident a glass of water to help wash the food down and then wait to see whether breathing improves
- B. Perform a blind finger sweep of the resident's mouth to look for and remove the food that is stuck
- **✓ C. Perform abdominal thrusts until the object is expelled or the resident becomes unresponsive**
- D. Encourage coughing

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Inability to speak or cough with cyanosis indicates a severe airway obstruction, which is treated with abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive, when CPR is started. Fluids can worsen the obstruction, blind finger sweeps can push the object deeper, and a person with a complete obstruction cannot cough.

### PN-A05-032 · Multiple choice · Physiological Adaptation

**Topic:** Hyponatremia

**Stem:** A client's sodium level is 122 mEq/L. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN immediately?

- A. A dry mouth and thirst after physical therapy
- **✓ B. New confusion and a headache**
- C. Mild ankle swelling at the end of the day
- D. Urine output of 50 mL/hr

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Low sodium causes water to shift into brain cells, so new confusion and headache can signal cerebral edema, which may progress to seizures. Thirst after activity, mild end-of-day ankle swelling, and adequate urine output do not indicate a neurologic emergency.

### PN-A05-033 · Multiple choice · Physiological Adaptation

**Topic:** Disconnected chest tube

**Stem:** The chest tube of a client with a pneumothorax becomes disconnected from the drainage system. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Clamp the chest tube tightly for several hours until the provider arrives on the unit
- B. Remove the chest tube and cover the site with a dry dressing
- C. Raise the drainage system above the level of the chest
- **✓ D. Place the end of the tube in sterile water**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Placing the end of the tube in sterile water creates a temporary water seal that keeps air from entering the pleural space until a new system is connected. Prolonged clamping can cause a tension pneumothorax, the tube is never removed by the nurse in this situation, and the drainage system must stay below the chest.

**Rationale:** A change in level of consciousness, such as restlessness or confusion, is the earliest and most sensitive sign of rising intracranial pressure and must be reported promptly. Cushing's triad, fixed dilated pupils, and abnormal posturing are late signs.

### PN-A05-039 · Multiple choice · Pharmacological Therapies

**Topic:** Warfarin and vitamin K intake

**Stem:** A client taking warfarin asks the LPN/VN whether they need to stop eating green leafy vegetables. Which response is correct?

- **✓ A. "Keep your vitamin K intake about the same each day rather than avoiding these foods."**
