### RN-A06-001 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Improving iron absorption

**Stem:** A client with iron deficiency anemia asks how to get the most iron from food. Which suggestion is best?

- **✓ A. Eat iron-rich foods, such as lean meat or beans, along with a source of vitamin C**
- B. Drink a large glass of milk with every iron-rich meal to help absorb the iron
- C. Have tea or coffee with meals to improve absorption
- D. Eat iron-rich foods only once a week

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Vitamin C increases absorption of iron, especially the nonheme iron in plant foods, so pairing iron-rich foods with foods such as citrus or tomatoes helps. Calcium in milk and tannins in tea and coffee reduce iron absorption, and iron-rich foods should be eaten regularly.

### RN-A06-002 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Nutrition in a vegan diet

**Stem:** A client who follows a vegan diet asks which nutrient is most likely to need a supplement or fortified food. What is the nurse's best response?

- A. Vitamin C, since fruits and vegetables do not provide enough of it to meet the daily needs of adults
- B. Carbohydrates
- **✓ C. Vitamin B12, since it is found mainly in animal foods**
- D. Fiber

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Vitamin B12 comes almost entirely from animal products, so people who follow a vegan diet need fortified foods or a supplement to prevent anemia and nerve damage. Fruits and vegetables are rich in vitamin C, and plant-based diets usually provide plenty of carbohydrates and fiber.

### RN-A06-003 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Perineal care for an uncircumcised client

**Stem:** The nurse is providing perineal care for an uncircumcised male client. Which action is correct?

- A. Retract the foreskin, clean the glans, and leave the foreskin retracted to let the area dry
- B. Clean only the outside of the foreskin, without retracting it at any time
- C. Use alcohol wipes on the glans
- **✓ D. Gently retract the foreskin, clean the glans, and return the foreskin**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** The foreskin is gently retracted so the glans can be cleaned and then returned to its normal position, because a foreskin left retracted can constrict the glans (paraphimosis). Cleaning only the outside leaves secretions underneath, and alcohol irritates the delicate tissue.

### RN-A06-004 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Mouth care at the end of life

**Stem:** A client receiving end-of-life care breathes through the mouth and has a very dry mouth. Which comfort measure is most appropriate?

- A. Give large sips of water every hour even though the client has trouble swallowing
- **✓ B. Moisten the mouth often with swabs and apply lip balm**
- C. Clean the mouth with lemon-glycerin swabs several times a day
- D. Apply petroleum jelly inside the mouth to keep it moist

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Frequent moistening with swabs or a water spray and a lip moisturizer relieve dryness safely. Large sips can cause aspiration in a client who has trouble swallowing, lemon-glycerin swabs dry and irritate the mouth, and petroleum jelly is not used inside the mouth because it can be aspirated.

### RN-A06-005 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Helping a client with low vision eat

**Stem:** A client with low vision is having difficulty finding food on the meal tray. Which action by the nurse is most helpful?

- A. Put all of the foods together in one bowl to make them easier to find
- B. Feed the client every meal so all of the food is eaten
- **✓ C. Describe where each food is on the plate using the positions of a clock face**
- D. Leave the tray and check back in an hour

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Describing food locations by clock position, such as "meat at 6 o'clock," lets the client eat independently and with dignity. Mixing foods together is unappetizing, feeding the client removes independence, and leaving without explaining the tray does not solve the problem.

### RN-A06-006 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Denture care

**Stem:** The nurse is caring for a client's dentures at bedtime. Which action is correct?

- A. Soak the dentures in very hot water overnight to kill bacteria and remove all stains completely
- B. Wrap the dentures in a paper towel
- C. Leave the dentures dry on the tray
- **✓ D. Store the dentures in a labeled cup with cool water or soaking solution**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Dentures are stored in a labeled denture cup with cool water or a soaking solution so they do not dry out, warp, or get lost. Hot water can warp them, and dentures wrapped in paper towels or left on trays are often thrown away by mistake.

### RN-A06-007 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Bedpan use after hip fracture

**Stem:** A client with a new hip fracture is on bed rest and needs to void. Which approach is correct?

- A. Use a regular bedpan after the client sits fully upright with the hips flexed to 90 degrees
- **✓ B. Use a fracture pan, slid under the buttocks with minimal hip movement**
- C. Use a bedside commode after helping the client stand and pivot on the injured leg
- D. Use an adult brief only

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A fracture pan is flatter than a regular bedpan, so it can be placed with little lifting or hip movement, reducing pain and the risk of displacing the fracture. Sitting fully upright on a regular bedpan or standing on the injured leg is unsafe, and relying only on a brief does not support toileting and increases skin breakdown.

### RN-A06-008 · Select all that apply · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Relieving dyspnea at the end of life

**Stem:** A client receiving hospice care has dyspnea. Which comfort measures can the nurse use? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Use a fan to blow cool air across the face**
- **✓ B. Raise the head of the bed or position the client upright**
- **✓ C. Give prescribed opioids for breathlessness**
- **✓ D. Keep the room calm and quiet**
- E. Encourage the client to walk in the hallway to build stamina
- F. Keep the room very warm with the windows closed

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** Moving cool air across the face, upright positioning, prescribed low-dose opioids, and a calm environment reduce the sensation of breathlessness. Walking to build stamina is not a goal at the end of life and increases dyspnea, and a hot, closed room usually worsens the feeling of air hunger.

### RN-A06-009 · Select all that apply · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Independent eating after a stroke

**Stem:** A client with right-sided weakness and loss of the right visual field after a stroke is learning to eat independently. Which actions help? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Provide adaptive utensils with built-up handles**
- **✓ B. Use a plate guard or scoop dish**
- **✓ C. Place food within the client's intact (left) visual field**
- **✓ D. Remind the client to turn the head to scan the whole tray**
- E. Feed the client to save time
- F. Place the tray on the client's right side

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** Adaptive utensils and plate guards make self-feeding easier, placing food in the intact visual field helps the client see it, and teaching the client to turn the head compensates for the field loss. Feeding the client removes independence, and food placed on the affected side may be missed.

### RN-A06-012 · Highlight · Basic Care and Comfort

**Topic:** Pureed diet

**Stem:** A client with dysphagia is prescribed a pureed diet. Highlight the items on the meal tray that the client should not receive.

Key (highlight): Toast with butter; Raw carrot sticks; Cooked white rice

- s1: Pureed chicken
- s2: Toast with butter
- s3: Smooth applesauce
- s4: Raw carrot sticks
- s5: Pudding
- s6: Cooked white rice
- s7: Smooth mashed potatoes

**Answer:** Toast with butter; Raw carrot sticks; Cooked white rice

**Rationale:** A pureed diet includes only smooth foods without lumps that need no chewing, such as pureed meats, smooth applesauce, pudding, and smooth mashed potatoes. Toast, raw vegetables, and loose grains such as rice need chewing or can scatter in the mouth and be aspirated.

### RN-A06-013 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Lead exposure during renovation

**Stem:** Parents are renovating their 1950s home, where their 2-year-old lives. Which recommendation is most important to reduce the child's lead exposure?

- **✓ A. Have the child stay elsewhere during the renovation**
- B. Sand the old painted walls while the child naps in another room of the house
- C. Sweep paint dust dry each evening with a household broom to keep floors clean
- D. Give the child extra juice to flush lead from the body after the work is done

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Homes built before 1978 may contain lead paint, and renovation creates lead dust that young children swallow through hand-to-mouth activity. Keeping the child away during the work, using lead-safe certified contractors, and wet cleaning reduce exposure. Sanding nearby and dry sweeping spread dust, and juice does not remove lead.

### RN-A06-014 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Carbon monoxide safety

**Stem:** A family uses a gas furnace and a fuel-burning space heater in winter. Which teaching best protects the family from carbon monoxide poisoning?

- A. Open a window slightly when headaches occur, since headaches are the only warning sign
- B. Use a charcoal grill indoors only when the space heater is turned off
- **✓ C. Install carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas and have the furnace inspected each year**
- D. Rely on the smell of gas to detect carbon monoxide

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless, so alarms near sleeping areas and yearly inspection of fuel-burning appliances are the best protection. Headache is only one possible early symptom and is easily missed, charcoal grills must never be used indoors, and the smell of natural gas does not detect carbon monoxide.

### RN-A06-015 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Preventing furniture tip-over injuries

**Stem:** The nurse is teaching the parents of a toddler about home injury prevention. Which instruction is most important for preventing furniture tip-over injuries?

- A. Place the television on top of a tall dresser so the toddler cannot reach the cords or the buttons
- B. Keep toys on the top shelves
- C. Leave the drawers open
- **✓ D. Anchor dressers, bookcases, and televisions to the wall**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Toddlers climb furniture and pull on drawers, so anchoring heavy furniture and televisions to the wall prevents them from tipping over. A television on a tall dresser increases the risk, toys on high shelves encourage climbing, and open drawers act as steps.

### RN-A06-016 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Poison prevention at home

**Stem:** Which statement by a parent shows correct understanding of poison prevention at home?

- **✓ A. "I'll keep cleaning products in their original containers, locked up."**
- B. "I'll put the leftover bleach in an empty juice bottle so it's easy to pour."
- C. "I'll make my child vomit right away if they swallow something harmful."
- D. "I'll store medicines in my purse."

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Chemicals stay in their original labeled containers and are stored locked and out of reach. Food or drink containers invite children to drink the contents, inducing vomiting is not recommended, and purses are easy for children to open. Parents should call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 for any suspected poisoning.

### RN-A06-017 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Cleaning a blood spill

**Stem:** A small amount of blood has spilled on the floor of a client's room. What should the nurse do?

- A. Wipe it up with a paper towel without gloves, since the amount of blood is very small
- **✓ B. Wear gloves, absorb it, and clean with an approved disinfectant**
- C. Cover it with a towel and wait for housekeeping to arrive tomorrow morning
- D. Spray it with water and mop the area until the floor looks clean

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Blood spills are cleaned promptly while wearing gloves: the blood is absorbed and the area is cleaned and disinfected with an EPA-registered disinfectant or diluted bleach per policy. Any amount of blood can transmit bloodborne pathogens, leaving it creates a hazard, and water alone does not disinfect.

### RN-A06-018 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Transporting a client on airborne precautions

**Stem:** A client on airborne precautions for tuberculosis must go to radiology. What should the nurse do?

- A. Have staff wear surgical masks while the client stays unmasked
- **✓ B. Have the client wear a surgical mask and notify radiology in advance**
- C. Delay all imaging until the client is no longer contagious
- D. Take the client quickly through the busiest hallway

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Clients on airborne precautions leave the room only when necessary. During transport, the client wears a surgical mask to contain respiratory particles, and the receiving department is notified so it can take precautions. Staff need N95 respirators rather than surgical masks when caring for the client, needed imaging is not delayed, and busy areas should be avoided.

### RN-A06-019 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Disposing of leftover opioids

**Stem:** A client asks how to get rid of leftover oxycodone tablets after recovering from surgery. What is the nurse's best response?

- A. "Keep them in the medicine cabinet in case a family member has pain later on this year."
- B. "Give them to a friend who has back pain."
- C. "Throw the bottle in the trash."
- **✓ D. "Take them to a drug take-back site, such as a pharmacy kiosk."**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Drug take-back sites are the preferred way to dispose of unused opioids. If none is available, FDA guidance allows some opioids, including oxycodone, to be flushed. Keeping leftover opioids or giving them to others leads to misuse and accidental poisoning, and a full bottle in the trash can be found and used.

### RN-A06-020 · Select all that apply · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Purposeful hourly rounding

**Stem:** The unit uses purposeful hourly rounding to prevent falls. Which actions are part of each round? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Ask about and address pain**
- **✓ B. Offer help with toileting**
- **✓ C. Help the client change position if needed**
- **✓ D. Place the call light and personal items within reach**
- E. Turn off the bed alarm to let the client rest
- F. Raise all four side rails

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

### RN-A06-021 · Select all that apply · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Safe sleep for infants

**Stem:** The nurse is teaching parents about safe sleep for their newborn. Which instructions are correct? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Place the infant on the back for every sleep**
- **✓ B. Use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet**
- **✓ C. Keep pillows, blankets, and bumpers out of the crib**
- **✓ D. Share the room, but not the bed, with the infant**
- E. Place the infant on the stomach after feedings
- F. Let the infant sleep in a car seat in the crib

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** Safe sleep practices to reduce sudden unexpected infant death include back sleeping, a firm, flat surface, a crib free of soft items, and room sharing without bed sharing. Stomach sleeping increases risk, and sitting devices such as car seats are not used for routine sleep.

### RN-A06-023 · Drop-down cloze · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Preventing scald burns

**Stem:** Complete the sentence by choosing from the lists of options.

Template: To prevent scald burns in a home with young children, the water heater should be set no higher than ___1___, and bath water should be tested with ___2___ before the child gets in.

- Blank 1: **✓ 120°F (49°C)** / 140°F (60°C) / 160°F (71°C)
- Blank 2: **✓ the wrist or elbow** / the child's foot / the fingertips only

**Answer:** 1: 120°F (49°C); 2: the wrist or elbow

**Rationale:** At 120°F (49°C) or lower, water takes longer to cause a serious burn, giving time to react. The inner wrist or elbow is more sensitive to heat than the fingertips, and a child's foot should never be used to test the water.
