## PN-C36: Orthostatic hypotension after starting doxazosin

System: Cardiovascular · Discipline: Pharmacology

**Scenario:** An 80-year-old resident of a long-term care facility started doxazosin 3 days ago for high blood pressure and urinary symptoms. This morning, the resident became dizzy and nearly fell when getting out of bed.

**Nurses' notes, 0700:** Lightheaded when standing and sat back down on the bed. No injury. Alert and oriented. Drank about 800 mL of fluid yesterday.

**Orthostatic vital signs, 0710:** Lying: BP 138/80 mm Hg, HR 72/min. Sitting: BP 118/70 mm Hg, HR 84/min. Standing for 1 minute: BP 102/62 mm Hg, HR 96/min, dizzy.

**Medications:** Doxazosin 2 mg by mouth at bedtime, started 3 days ago. Lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily at 0900. Furosemide 20 mg by mouth daily at 0900.

- s4: HR 72/min lying and 96/min standing

**Answer:** Dizzy and nearly fell when getting out of bed; Lightheaded when standing; BP 138/80 lying and 102/62 standing; HR 72/min lying and 96/min standing; Drank about 800 mL of fluid yesterday; Doxazosin started 3 days ago

**Rationale:** A systolic drop of 36 mm Hg with a rising heart rate and dizziness on standing is orthostatic hypotension, which caused a near fall. Low fluid intake and a newly started alpha-blocker are likely causes. The lack of injury and normal orientation are reassuring.

## PN-C37: Undertreated hypothyroidism in an older adult

System: Endocrine · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** The home health LPN/VN visits a 77-year-old client for wound care. The client's daughter says the client has seemed "slowed down" for the past 2 months.

**Nurses' notes:** Speaks slowly and sleeps 12 hours a day. Always cold and wears a sweater in a warm room. Skin dry and rough, and hair thinning. Constipated, with a bowel movement every 4 days. Weight up 8 lb (3.6 kg) in 2 months without eating more. Puffy face and eyelids. Takes levothyroxine "when I remember," with breakfast and a calcium supplement.

**Vital signs:** T 96.8°F (36°C), HR 54/min, RR 14/min, BP 132/88 mm Hg.

**Laboratory results from last month:** TSH 18.2 mIU/L (0.4–4.0). Free T4 0.5 ng/dL (0.8–1.8).

## PN-C38: Complications after cataract surgery

System: Sensory · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** A 70-year-old client had cataract surgery with a lens implant in the right eye this morning at an outpatient surgery center and is preparing to go home with a spouse.

**Nurses' notes, 1100:** Eye shield in place over the right eye. Mild scratchiness, with pain 2/10. Vision blurry in the right eye.

**Nurses' notes, 1300:** Reports sudden, severe pain in the right eye, 8/10, and nausea. The pain did not improve 30 minutes after acetaminophen.

**Discharge instructions:** Prednisolone acetate and moxifloxacin eye drops, 1 drop each, four times a day. Wear the eye shield at night for 1 week. Avoid bending at the waist, lifting more than 10 lb, and rubbing the eye. Call for pain not relieved by acetaminophen, vision loss, flashes of light, or nausea. Follow-up visit tomorrow morning.

## PN-C39: Comfort care at the end of life in long-term care

System: Integrated · Discipline: Fundamentals

**Scenario:** A 93-year-old resident of a long-term care facility with advanced dementia has received hospice care for 3 weeks. The resident has a do-not-resuscitate order and a POLST indicating comfort-focused treatment.

**Nurses' notes, 0600:** Unresponsive to voice. Breathing irregular, with 20-second pauses and a gurgling sound. Hands and feet cool and mottled. No urine output for 10 hours. Grimaces and moans when turned. Mouth dry, with crusted lips.

**Vital signs, 0600:** HR 118/min and weak, RR 8 to 24/min and irregular, BP 82/46 mm Hg, SpO2 unable to obtain.

**Prescriptions:** Morphine oral solution 5 mg sublingually every 2 hours as needed for pain or labored breathing. Glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg subcutaneously every 4 hours as needed for secretions. Mouth care every 2 hours. Hospice nurse on call 24 hours a day.

**Family:** The resident's son lives 2 hours away and asked to be called if there is any change.

### PN-C39-01 · Highlight · Physiological Adaptation · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that indicate the resident is likely in the last hours of life.

- s4: Hands and feet cool and mottled
- s5: No urine output for 10 hours

**Answer:** Unresponsive to voice; Breathing irregular, with 20-second pauses; Gurgling sound with breathing; Hands and feet cool and mottled; No urine output for 10 hours

**Rationale:** Unresponsiveness, irregular breathing with apnea, noisy secretions, cool mottled extremities, and no urine output are signs that death is near. Grimacing with turning and a dry mouth are comfort needs that require care but are not, by themselves, signs that death is imminent.

## PN-C40: Chest pain in a long-term care resident

System: Cardiovascular · Discipline: Management of Care

**Scenario:** At 1930, a 79-year-old resident of a long-term care facility with diabetes and hypertension tells the LPN/VN charge nurse about "pressure" in the chest and nausea that began 20 minutes ago while watching television.

**Nurses' notes, 1930:** Pale and sweaty. Rates the chest pressure 7/10, spreading to the left jaw. Nauseated. Short of breath when talking. Full code.

**Vital signs, 1930:** HR 104/min and irregular, RR 24/min, BP 150/92 mm Hg, SpO2 92% on room air. Fingerstick glucose 186 mg/dL.

**Facility chest pain protocol:** Call 911. Notify the RN supervisor and the provider. Give nitroglycerin 0.4 mg sublingually every 5 minutes, up to 3 doses, if the systolic BP is above 90 mm Hg. Give aspirin 324 mg to chew if there is no allergy. Apply oxygen if the SpO2 is below 90%. Stay with the resident.
