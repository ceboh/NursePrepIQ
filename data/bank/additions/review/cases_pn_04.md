## PN-C16: Acute stroke in a long-term care resident

System: Neurologic · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** A 76-year-old resident of a long-term care facility with atrial fibrillation and hypertension is found by the assistive personnel at 1015 with slurred speech. The resident was last seen acting normally at 0930 during breakfast.

**Nurses' notes, 1020:** Right side of the face droops. Right arm drifts down when both arms are held out. Speech slurred, with trouble finding words. Alert and follows commands. Able to swallow saliva. No seizure activity witnessed.

**Vital signs and glucose, 1020:** T 98.4°F (36.9°C), HR 96/min and irregular, RR 18/min, BP 176/94 mm Hg, SpO2 95% on room air. Fingerstick glucose 118 mg/dL.

**Medication record:** Apixaban 5 mg by mouth twice daily; the resident refused the last two doses. Lisinopril 20 mg by mouth daily.

**Facility stroke protocol:** Call 911. Notify the RN supervisor and provider. Record the time the resident was last known well. Check glucose. Keep NPO until a swallow screen is done. Stay with the resident and monitor neurologic status.

**Answer:** Right side of the face droops; Right arm drifts down; Speech slurred, with trouble finding words; HR 96/min and irregular; Refused the last two doses of apixaban; Last seen acting normally at 0930

**Rationale:** Sudden facial droop, arm drift, and speech changes are signs of stroke, and an irregular pulse with missed anticoagulant doses increases the risk of an embolic stroke. The time last known well determines treatment options. A normal glucose and oxygen saturation and the ability to follow commands do not need immediate action.

## PN-C17: Elevated INR and bleeding in a client taking warfarin

System: Hematologic · Discipline: Pharmacology

**Scenario:** A 70-year-old client with a mechanical heart valve who takes warfarin comes to the anticoagulation clinic for a routine INR check. The client started an antibiotic 5 days ago for a sinus infection.

**Nurses' notes:** Reports that the gums bleed when brushing and that a nosebleed yesterday stopped after 10 minutes of pressure. Several bruises on both forearms. Denies black stools, blood in the urine, or headache. Has been eating less because of the sinus infection.

**Laboratory results:** INR 5.8 (goal 2.5–3.5). Hemoglobin 12.9 g/dL (12–16).

**Medications:** Warfarin 5 mg by mouth daily. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole by mouth twice daily, started 5 days ago.

### PN-C17-01 · Highlight · Pharmacological Therapies · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that require follow-up.

Key (highlight): Gums bleed when brushing; Nosebleed yesterday; Several bruises on both forearms; Eating less because of the sinus infection; INR 5.8; Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole started 5 days ago

- s1: Gums bleed when brushing
- s2: Nosebleed yesterday
- s3: Several bruises on both forearms
- s4: Eating less because of the sinus infection
- s5: INR 5.8
- s6: Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole started 5 days ago
- s7: Denies black stools
- s8: Hemoglobin 12.9 g/dL

**Answer:** Gums bleed when brushing; Nosebleed yesterday; Several bruises on both forearms; Eating less because of the sinus infection; INR 5.8; Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole started 5 days ago

**Rationale:** Minor bleeding signs and an INR well above the goal show excessive anticoagulation. The new antibiotic, which increases warfarin's effect, and lower vitamin K intake from eating less explain the rise. The absence of black stools and a normal hemoglobin suggest no major bleeding.

## PN-C18: Toddler with gastroenteritis and dehydration

System: Fluids & Electrolytes · Discipline: Pediatrics

**Scenario:** A 2-year-old is brought to the pediatric clinic with vomiting and diarrhea for 2 days. A sibling at home had similar symptoms last week.

**Nurses' notes:** Fussy but consolable. Mucous membranes sticky. Tears present when crying but fewer than usual. Capillary refill 2 seconds. Weight 11.4 kg, down from 12 kg at a visit 2 weeks ago.

**Vital signs:** T 100.2°F (37.9°C), HR 132/min, RR 28/min.

## PN-C20: Insulin given to the wrong client

System: Endocrine · Discipline: Management of Care

**Scenario:** On a medical unit, an LPN/VN gives 10 units of rapid-acting insulin lispro at 0730 to the client in bed 2. Fifteen minutes later, the LPN/VN realizes the insulin was prescribed for the client in bed 1. The client in bed 2 has type 2 diabetes managed with diet and metformin and has not yet eaten breakfast.

**Nurses' notes, 0745:** Client in bed 2 is alert and oriented, with no symptoms. Breakfast tray arriving at 0800. The bar-code scanner was not used because it was charging.

**Glucose results:** Fingerstick glucose 142 mg/dL at 0700 and 128 mg/dL at 0745.

**Facility policy: medication errors:** Assess the client. Notify the RN and provider immediately. Monitor as the provider prescribes. Complete an event report. Disclose the error to the client according to policy.

**Answer:** 10 units of rapid-acting insulin lispro; Managed with diet and metformin; Has not yet eaten breakfast; Breakfast tray arriving at 0800; Glucose 128 mg/dL at 0745, down from 142

**Rationale:** A client who does not normally use insulin received a full dose of rapid-acting insulin before eating, and the glucose is already falling, so hypoglycemia is likely as the insulin peaks. Being alert and symptom-free now does not mean the client will stay safe.
