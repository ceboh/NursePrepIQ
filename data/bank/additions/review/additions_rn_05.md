### RN-A05-001 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Grandiose delusions

**Stem:** A client with bipolar disorder tells the nurse, "I'm a famous surgeon, and I need to operate on the patient down the hall." What is the nurse's best response?

- **✓ A. "It sounds like you want to help. Let's talk about what's going on for you on the unit today."**
- B. "You are not a surgeon. You are a client in a hospital, and you need to accept that now."
- C. "Which hospital did you train at, and how long have you been operating?"
- D. "I'll let the surgeon know you'd like to help with that patient's care."

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Arguing with a delusion increases defensiveness, and asking for details or agreeing to pass the request along reinforces it. The nurse acknowledges the feeling behind the statement without confirming the false belief and redirects the conversation to reality-based topics.

### RN-A05-002 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Supporting a child with ADHD

**Stem:** The parents of a 7-year-old with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder ask how to help their child finish homework. Which suggestion is most helpful?

- A. Have the child do all of the homework at once in front of the television to stay relaxed
- **✓ B. Break homework into short tasks with brief breaks in a quiet space**
- C. Remove all privileges until every assignment is done
- D. Do the homework for the child

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Children with ADHD do best with short, structured tasks, frequent breaks, and few distractions, along with consistent routines and positive reinforcement. Long sessions with a television on increase distraction, removing all privileges is overly punitive, and doing the work for the child prevents learning.

### RN-A05-003 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

- B. Place the child in a busy room near the nurses' station for close observation
- **✓ C. Ask the parents about the child's routines and keep them as much as possible**

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Children with autism spectrum disorder often find change, noise, and unexpected touch distressing. Rotating nurses, a busy room, and frequent hugs can increase anxiety and behavioral problems.

### RN-A05-004 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Somatic symptom disorder

**Stem:** A client with somatic symptom disorder has had many visits for different physical complaints, and test results have been normal. Which approach by the nurse is most appropriate?

- A. Tell the client that the symptoms are imaginary, since all of the test results have been normal
- B. Encourage the client to request new diagnostic tests whenever a new symptom develops
- C. Avoid discussing the client's symptoms at all so that the client will stop focusing on them
- **✓ D. Acknowledge the symptoms and focus on coping and function**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** The symptoms and distress are real to the client. Care focuses on acknowledging them, scheduling regular visits with a consistent provider, and building coping skills and daily function rather than repeating tests. Calling the symptoms imaginary or refusing to discuss them damages trust, and repeated testing reinforces the focus on symptoms.

### RN-A05-005 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Religious fasting and diabetes

**Stem:** A client with type 2 diabetes who takes insulin plans to fast from dawn to sunset during Ramadan. What is the nurse's best response?

- A. "People with diabetes are never allowed to fast, so you will have to skip Ramadan this year."
- B. "You can keep taking your usual insulin doses at the usual times during the fast."
- C. "Fasting is fine as long as you stop checking your blood glucose until sunset."
- **✓ D. "Let's arrange a visit with your provider before Ramadan to adjust your medicines and glucose checks."**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** The nurse respects the client's religious practice and helps the client plan for safety. A visit before Ramadan allows the provider to assess risk, adjust insulin doses and timing, and plan glucose monitoring, which does not break the fast, along with clear guidance on when to end the fast for hypoglycemia. Many people with diabetes fast safely with planning, while unchanged insulin doses and skipping glucose checks risk hypoglycemia.

### RN-A05-007 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Factitious disorder imposed on another

**Stem:** A 3-year-old has had repeated hospital admissions for unexplained seizures and bleeding. The symptoms occur only when the child's mother is present and stop when staff supervise the child. Which condition should the nurse suspect?

- A. Separation anxiety disorder in the child
- **✓ B. Factitious disorder imposed on another**
- C. A rare genetic bleeding and seizure disorder
- D. Normal developmental behavior

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Symptoms that appear only in a caregiver's presence and stop when the child is separated from the caregiver suggest factitious disorder imposed on another, in which a caregiver fabricates or induces illness. It is a form of child abuse, so the nurse documents objective observations, keeps the child safe, and follows mandatory reporting requirements. The pattern does not fit separation anxiety, a genetic disorder, or normal development.

### RN-A05-008 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Depression in older adults

**Stem:** Over the past month, since the death of a spouse, an 80-year-old client has become forgetful, withdrawn, and slow to respond and often answers "I don't know" to questions. Which condition should the nurse suspect?

- A. Alzheimer's disease in its early stage
- B. Delirium caused by a urinary tract infection
- **✓ C. Depression**
- D. Normal age-related memory changes

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Depression in older adults can look like dementia, with poor concentration, memory complaints, withdrawal, and frequent "I don't know" answers, especially after a loss. Alzheimer's disease develops gradually over a longer time, and people with it often try to answer or cover gaps. Delirium has an acute onset with fluctuating attention, and normal aging does not cause withdrawal. The nurse screens for depression and reports the findings.

### RN-A05-009 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Traditional healing practices

**Stem:** During a clinic visit, the nurse notices evenly spaced, linear red marks on the back of a child who has had a cold. The parent explains that the family used coin rubbing, a traditional remedy. What should the nurse do?

- A. Report the parent to child protective services right away for physical abuse of the child
- B. Tell the parent that traditional remedies are harmful and must be stopped
- C. Ignore the marks and avoid mentioning them
- **✓ D. Recognize coining as a traditional healing practice and ask about the child's symptoms and care**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Coin rubbing (coining), used in several Southeast Asian cultures, produces linear red marks that can be mistaken for abuse. When the marks match the explanation, the nurse respects the practice, asks about the child's illness and other treatments, and makes sure the child receives needed care. Reporting abuse is not warranted by these findings alone, dismissing the practice damages trust, and ignoring the marks misses a chance to assess.

### RN-A05-010 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Signs that death is near

**Stem:** The family of a client receiving hospice care asks what changes to expect as death approaches. Which information should the nurse include?

- **✓ A. "Breathing may become irregular with pauses, and the hands and feet may look mottled."**
- B. "Breathing usually becomes deeper and more regular, and the skin becomes warm and pink all over."
- C. "Hearing is lost first, so talking to your loved one won't help."
- D. "Eating more will help."

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** As death approaches, breathing often becomes irregular with periods of apnea, and the extremities become cool and mottled as circulation decreases. Appetite naturally declines, and pushing food can cause discomfort or aspiration.

### RN-A05-011 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Flashbacks in posttraumatic stress disorder

**Stem:** A client with posttraumatic stress disorder suddenly looks terrified and says, "They're coming. I have to hide." The nurse recognizes a flashback. What should the nurse do?

- A. Leave the client alone in the room until the flashback ends on its own, and then return to talk
- **✓ B. Speak calmly, state the client's name and location, and help them notice things in the room**
- C. Ask the client to describe every detail of the original traumatic event right now while it is fresh
- D. Firmly grasp the client's shoulders

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Grounding techniques, such as calm orientation to the present and noticing what can be seen, heard, and touched, help the client return to the here and now. Leaving the client alone during a flashback is unsafe, pushing the client to recount the trauma can deepen distress, and unexpected touch may be perceived as a threat.

### RN-A05-013 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Motivational interviewing

**Stem:** A client who smokes says, "I know I should quit, but smoking is the only thing that helps me relax." Which response by the nurse reflects motivational interviewing?

- **✓ A. "Smoking helps you relax, and you also see reasons to quit. What would quitting mean for you?"**

- C. "Lots of people find other ways to relax, so that's not really a good reason."

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Motivational interviewing reflects both sides of the client's ambivalence and uses open-ended questions to draw out the client's own reasons for change. Lecturing about risks and dismissing the client's reason increase resistance, and waiting misses the chance to build motivation.

### RN-A05-014 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Stem:** The nurse is helping a client who has had suicidal thoughts develop a safety plan before discharge. Which elements belong in the plan? Select all that apply.

- **✓ B. Coping strategies the client can use alone**

### RN-A05-015 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

- **✓ C. Erosion of tooth enamel**
- **✓ D. Frequent trips to the bathroom right after meals**
- E. Lanugo over the trunk and face
- F. Body weight more than 20% below normal

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

### RN-A05-021 · Drop-down cloze · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** General adaptation syndrome

**Stem:** Complete the sentence by choosing from the lists of options.

Template: According to Selye's general adaptation syndrome, the body first responds to a stressor in the ___1___ stage, and prolonged stress without relief can lead to the ___2___ stage, when illness becomes more likely.

- Blank 1: **✓ alarm** / resistance / exhaustion
- Blank 2: **✓ exhaustion** / alarm / recovery

**Answer:** 1: alarm; 2: exhaustion

**Rationale:** In the alarm stage, the fight-or-flight response mobilizes the body. In the resistance stage, the body adapts to ongoing stress. If stress continues without relief, the exhaustion stage follows, when resources are depleted and the risk of illness rises.

### RN-A05-031 · Multiple choice · Basic Care and Comfort

- B. Give the client a large meal right before getting up to raise energy

**Rationale:** Counterpressure, position changes and walking, warm water, and patterned breathing reduce the perception of pain in labor.
