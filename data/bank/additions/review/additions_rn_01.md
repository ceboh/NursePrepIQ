### RN-A01-001 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: oncology unit

**Stem:** The nurse on an oncology unit receives report on four clients. Which client should the nurse assess first?

- **✓ A. A client 7 days after chemotherapy with a temperature of 101.3°F (38.5°C) and an absolute neutrophil count of 400/mm³**
- B. A client with metastatic breast cancer who is requesting a scheduled dose of oral morphine for pain rated 5/10
- C. A client receiving radiation to the neck who reports a sore throat and is asking for a softer diet tray
- D. A client with lymphoma who is scheduled for a bone marrow biopsy this afternoon and has questions about it

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Fever with severe neutropenia (ANC below 500/mm³) is an oncologic emergency that can progress to sepsis within hours; the client needs immediate assessment, cultures, and antibiotics. The client asking for scheduled morphine for moderate pain is stable and can be seen next. A sore throat during neck radiation is an expected effect that needs a diet change, not urgent care. Questions about a biopsy later in the day are a teaching need that can wait.

### RN-A01-004 · Select all that apply · Management of Care

**Topic:** Supervising assistive personnel

**Stem:** The nurse observes an assistive personnel (AP) caring for several clients. Which actions by the AP require the nurse to intervene? Select all that apply.

- A. Raising the head of the bed to 30 degrees for a client receiving a continuous tube feeding
- **✓ B. Taking a rectal temperature for a client with severe neutropenia**
- **✓ C. Using the same stethoscope for two clients on contact precautions without cleaning it**
- **✓ D. Helping a client 1 day after a posterior hip replacement sit in a low, soft armchair**
- E. Applying a gait belt before walking a client who is unsteady
- F. Offering the bedpan every 2 hours to a client with dementia who is incontinent

**Answer:** B, C, D

**Rationale:** Rectal temperatures are avoided in severe neutropenia because they can injure the mucosa and introduce infection. Equipment shared between clients on contact precautions must be dedicated or disinfected between uses. A low, soft chair flexes the hip beyond 90 degrees and risks dislocation after posterior hip replacement. Keeping the head of the bed at 30 degrees during tube feeding, using a gait belt for an unsteady client, and offering scheduled toileting are correct actions.

### RN-A01-005 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Informed consent with an interpreter

**Stem:** A client who speaks only Spanish is scheduled for surgery. The surgeon asks the client's 15-year-old daughter to interpret during the consent discussion. What should the nurse do?

- A. Let the daughter interpret, since she is bilingual and knows her parent's history
- B. Witness the signature, since obtaining consent is the surgeon's responsibility
- C. Have the client sign the English form and explain the details after surgery
- **✓ D. Request a qualified medical interpreter for the consent discussion**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Informed consent requires that the client understand the information, so a qualified medical interpreter should be used. Family members, especially minors, may omit or change information and are not appropriate interpreters for consent. Although the surgeon obtains consent, the nurse advocates for the client and should not witness a signature without valid understanding. Explaining details after surgery means consent was never informed.

### RN-A01-006 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** POLST and comfort-focused care

**Stem:** A client with end-stage COPD has a POLST form that indicates comfort-focused treatment and no intubation. The client becomes severely short of breath. What should the nurse do?

- A. Call the rapid response team and prepare for intubation, since the client is now in acute respiratory distress
- **✓ B. Provide comfort measures, such as upright positioning, a fan, and prescribed medication for dyspnea, consistent with the POLST**
- C. Ask the family whether they want the client intubated, since the client is too short of breath to decide now
- D. Withhold all treatment, including oxygen, since the POLST limits the client's care to comfort measures only

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A POLST is a set of portable medical orders that reflects the client's wishes. Comfort-focused treatment includes relieving dyspnea with positioning, a fan, oxygen if it adds comfort, and prescribed medications such as low-dose opioids. Preparing for intubation contradicts the client's orders. The family cannot override the client's documented wishes. Comfort care does not mean withholding treatments that relieve suffering.

### RN-A01-007 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Communicating diagnostic results

**Stem:** A client asks the nurse whether the biopsy shows cancer. The result is in the chart, but the provider has not yet discussed it with the client. What is the nurse's best response?

- A. "Yes, the report does show cancer, but your provider will explain what it means for your treatment options."
- B. "I'm not allowed to tell you anything about your results, so please don't ask me again."
- C. "Don't worry about the results right now. Try to get some rest, and we can talk later."
- **✓ D. "Your provider will go over the results with you. I will let them know you would like to talk today."**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Delivering a new diagnosis is the provider's responsibility; the nurse advocates for the client by making sure the provider knows the client wants to discuss the results promptly. Sharing the diagnosis before the provider can answer questions about it is outside the nurse's role. Refusing to engage and false reassurance both block communication and ignore the client's concern.

### RN-A01-009 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Chain of command

**Stem:** A client's oxygen saturation drops to 86% with walking, and the nurse believes the planned discharge today is unsafe. The provider declines to change the plan. What should the nurse do next?

- A. Discharge the client as prescribed and document the discussion with the provider
- B. Tell the client that the provider is making a mistake and should be replaced
- **✓ C. Notify the charge nurse or supervisor following the facility's chain of command**
- D. Cancel the discharge order in the electronic record until the provider reconsiders

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** When the nurse believes a plan is unsafe and the provider does not respond to the concern, the nurse advocates for the client by using the chain of command. ### RN-A01-010 · Drop-down cloze · Management of Care

**Topic:** Informed consent roles

**Stem:** Complete the sentence by choosing from the lists of options.

Template: The ___1___ is responsible for explaining the procedure, its risks and benefits, and the alternatives, and the nurse's signature as a witness confirms that ___2___.

- Blank 1: **✓ provider who will perform the procedure** / nurse assigned to the client / unit secretary
- Blank 2: **✓ the client signed voluntarily and appeared competent** / the client fully understands every possible risk / the procedure is medically necessary

**Answer:** 1: provider who will perform the procedure; 2: the client signed voluntarily and appeared competent

**Rationale:** The provider performing the procedure obtains informed consent by explaining the procedure, risks, benefits, and alternatives. The nurse's witness signature confirms that the signature is the client's, that it was given voluntarily, and that the client appeared competent. ### RN-A01-011 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Confidentiality of electronic records

**Stem:** A nurse who is not assigned to a well-known local politician opens the politician's electronic health record out of curiosity. How should this action be described?

- **✓ A. A privacy violation, because the nurse has no care-related reason to view the record**
- B. Acceptable, since any nurse working on the unit may need to care for the client in an emergency
- C. Acceptable, as long as the nurse does not share any of the information with people outside the hospital
- D. A documentation error that should be corrected by entering a late note in the client's record

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Health care workers may access only the information they need for their job. Viewing a record out of curiosity is a privacy violation even if nothing is shared, and access is usually audited. A possible future emergency does not justify access now, and the event is not a documentation error that a late entry can fix.

### RN-A01-013 · Select all that apply · Management of Care

**Topic:** Client rights: refusing a blood transfusion

**Stem:** A competent adult client who is a Jehovah's Witness refuses a blood transfusion after surgery. Which actions by the nurse are appropriate? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Notify the provider of the client's refusal**
- **✓ B. Document the refusal and the information given to the client**
- **✓ C. Ask which alternatives the client will accept, such as volume expanders**
- D. Give the transfusion if the client becomes unconscious, because consent is then implied
- E. Ask the client's family to persuade the client to accept the blood

**Answer:** A, B, C

**Rationale:** A competent adult has the right to refuse treatment. The nurse notifies the provider, documents the refusal and the information provided, and explores acceptable alternatives, since many clients accept volume expanders or other blood-sparing measures. A known, informed refusal remains valid if the client later loses consciousness, so implied consent does not apply. Pressuring the client through family members does not respect autonomy.

### RN-A01-014 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: labor and delivery

**Stem:** The nurse on a labor and delivery unit receives report on four clients. Which client should the nurse see first?

- **✓ A. A client receiving oxytocin whose contractions are coming every 90 seconds and last 80 seconds**
- B. A client in early labor at 3 cm who is asking for an epidural for pain rated 6/10 on the numeric scale
- C. A client 1 hour after a vaginal birth with a firm fundus and a moderate amount of lochia
- D. A client at 37 weeks with mild ankle swelling and a BP of 128/78 mm Hg

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Contractions every 90 seconds that last 80 seconds leave only about 10 seconds of uterine relaxation and add up to more than 5 contractions in 10 minutes. This tachysystole reduces placental perfusion and requires immediate action, such as reducing or stopping the oxytocin per protocol and assessing the fetal heart rate. An epidural request, a stable postpartum assessment, and mild edema with normal BP can follow.

### RN-A01-024 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Home oxygen safety

**Stem:** The nurse is reinforcing home oxygen safety. Which client statement indicates understanding?

- A. "I can use petroleum jelly on my lips while the oxygen is running."
- B. "I'll store the extra tanks in the closed closet next to the furnace."
- **✓ C. "I'll keep the oxygen well away from the gas stove and any candles."**
- D. "My husband can smoke in the room if I turn the oxygen down a little."

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Oxygen supports combustion, so it must be kept away from open flames and heat sources. Petroleum-based products are flammable, and a water-based lubricant should be used instead. Tanks should be stored upright in a well-ventilated area away from heat. No one should smoke near oxygen at any flow rate.

### RN-A01-028 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

- **✓ C. Allowing alcohol-based skin prep to dry completely before draping**

### RN-A01-033 · Select all that apply · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Sharps disposal at home

**Stem:** The nurse is reinforcing teaching about disposing of insulin syringes at home. Which instructions are correct? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Use an FDA-cleared sharps container or a heavy-duty plastic household container with a screw-on lid**

- E. Place loose syringes in the regular household trash
