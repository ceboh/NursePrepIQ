### PN-A02-001 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: pediatric clinic

**Stem:** The LPN/VN in a pediatric clinic is bringing children to exam rooms. Which child should the provider see first?

- **✓ A. A 4-year-old who is drooling, sitting leaning forward, and speaking in a muffled voice**
- B. A 2-year-old with a runny nose, a cough, and a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C)
- C. A 6-year-old with an itchy rash on both arms after a weekend camping trip
- D. A 10-year-old who is here for a sports physical and has no complaints

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Drooling, a forward-leaning (tripod) position, and a muffled voice suggest epiglottitis or another upper airway obstruction, which can quickly become complete; the provider is notified at once and no one should examine the child's throat. A mild cold, an itchy rash, and a routine physical can wait.

### PN-A02-002 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: postpartum unit

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a postpartum unit is collecting data on four clients. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. A client 1 day after birth whose fundus is firm and one fingerbreadth below the umbilicus
- B. A client who has afterpains while breastfeeding
- **✓ C. A client 2 hours after birth who has soaked a pad in 15 minutes**
- D. A client with lochia rubra on day 2

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Soaking a perineal pad in 15 minutes is excessive bleeding that may signal postpartum hemorrhage and needs immediate action. A firm fundus one fingerbreadth below the umbilicus on day 1, afterpains during breastfeeding, and lochia rubra during the first 3 days are expected findings.

### PN-A02-003 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritizing a medication pass

**Stem:** During the morning medication pass in a long-term care facility, which scheduled medication should the LPN/VN give first?

- A. A daily multivitamin for a resident who is scheduled to receive it at 0900 today
- B. A calcium supplement for a resident that is scheduled to be given with lunch
- C. A stool softener due at 0900
- **✓ D. Rapid-acting insulin for a resident whose breakfast tray just arrived**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Rapid-acting insulin is time-critical: it is given within about 15 minutes of a meal so its action matches the rise in blood glucose, and delays can cause hyperglycemia followed by hypoglycemia. A multivitamin, a stool softener, and a calcium supplement due later are not time-critical.

### PN-A02-004 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Questions about tissue donation

**Stem:** The family of a client who is dying asks the LPN/VN whether the client's corneas could be donated. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Explain that people who die after a long illness are never able to donate any tissue
- **✓ B. Notify the RN so the organ procurement organization can be contacted**
- C. Tell the family that only the client's provider is allowed to discuss donation at all
- D. Ask the family to wait until after death and then call the donation agency themselves

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Hospitals refer clients who are dying or have died to the organ procurement organization, whose trained staff talk with families about donation. The LPN/VN passes the family's question to the RN so the referral is made. Many people who die after a long illness can still donate corneas or other tissue, discussion is not limited to the provider, and the family should not have to arrange it themselves.

### PN-A02-005 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Advocating for pain relief

**Stem:** A client's pain is still 8/10 one hour after the prescribed oral opioid. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Tell the client that the medicine needs more time to start working fully
- B. Give the next dose early, since the client is still having severe pain
- **✓ C. Report the unrelieved pain to the RN so that the provider can change the plan**
- D. Document the pain rating and recheck the client at the end of the shift

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** An oral opioid usually reaches its peak effect within about an hour, so severe pain at that point means the plan is not working. The LPN/VN advocates for the client by reporting the unrelieved pain so the provider can adjust the prescription. Asking the client to wait longer or delaying a recheck leaves the pain untreated, and giving a dose early is outside the prescription.

### PN-A02-006 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Faxing client records

- A. Send the records to the number on an old referral form without calling to confirm it
- B. Leave the faxed pages on the machine for pickup
- C. Fax the entire chart to be safe

### PN-A02-007 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Respecting a client's wish for privacy

**Stem:** A competent client asks the LPN/VN not to tell their adult children about a new cancer diagnosis. Later, one of the children asks the LPN/VN about the test results. What should the LPN/VN say?

- A. "Since you're family, I can tell you, but please keep the results to yourself."
- **✓ B. "I'm not able to share that. It would be best to talk with your parent."**
- C. "The results were normal, so there is nothing at all for you to worry about."
- D. "Ask the provider, who will tell you."

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A competent client decides who receives their health information, so the LPN/VN does not share the results and suggests the child talk with the client. Being family does not override the client's wishes, giving false information violates honesty and trust, and sending the child to the provider wrongly suggests the provider will disclose what the client asked to keep private.

### PN-A02-008 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Short staffing in long-term care

**Stem:** The LPN/VN is the charge nurse on a long-term care unit. One of three assistive personnel (AP) has called in sick, and no replacement is available. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Redistribute care by resident needs, keeping essential care first**
- B. Divide the absent AP's residents evenly between the other two APs without changing any tasks
- C. Cancel all baths and walks for every resident on the unit for the rest of the day
- D. Ask the residents' families to come in and give all of the residents' personal care today

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** When staff are short, the charge nurse notifies the supervisor and reassigns care based on residents' needs, making sure essential care such as toileting, repositioning, feeding, and safety checks is done and adjusting less urgent tasks. Dividing residents evenly ignores differences in needs, canceling all ambulation harms residents, and families cannot be required to provide care.

### PN-A02-009 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Stem:** A resident's daughter tells the LPN/VN charge nurse that her mother was left in a wet brief for hours last night. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- **✓ C. Listen to the concern, check on the resident, and explain how it will be followed up**

### PN-A02-010 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Falsified documentation by an AP

**Stem:** The LPN/VN sees an assistive personnel (AP) recording vital signs for several residents before taking them. What should the LPN/VN do?

- C. Remind the AP to be more careful next time

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Recording data that were not measured is falsification and puts residents at risk because changes would be missed. The LPN/VN stops the practice, makes sure the vital signs are actually measured, and reports the behavior. Stable past readings do not justify it, a reminder does not address falsification, and waiting leaves false data in the record.

### PN-A02-016 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

### PN-A02-018 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Revoking a do-not-resuscitate order

**Stem:** A competent client who has a do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order tells the LPN/VN, "I've changed my mind. I want everything done if my heart stops." What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Explain that a DNR order cannot be changed once it has been signed by the provider
- B. Ask the client's family whether they agree
- C. Document the statement and wait
- **✓ D. Notify the RN and provider right away so the order can be changed**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A competent client can change their decisions about resuscitation at any time. The LPN/VN reports the request immediately so the provider can talk with the client and update the order, because the existing DNR order stays in effect until it is changed. The family's agreement is not required, and waiting could lead to care that is against the client's current wishes.

### PN-A02-019 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

- B. A resident who needs a two-person transfer with a mechanical lift and is often combative
- C. A resident with severe dysphagia who needs to be fed slowly with thickened liquids

### PN-A02-020 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Reviewing remote monitoring data

**Stem:** The LPN/VN reviews remote monitoring data sent from clients' homes. Which reading should the LPN/VN report to the RN first?

- A. A fasting blood glucose of 132 mg/dL in a client with type 2 diabetes who takes metformin
- B. A blood pressure of 142/88 mm Hg in a client who has had hypertension for many years
- **✓ C. SpO2 84% in a client with COPD whose usual level is 90%**

### PN-A02-021 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Cost-effective use of supplies

**Stem:** Which action by the LPN/VN reflects cost-effective use of resources?

- A. Opening a new sterile kit for each step of a clean dressing change
- B. Using sterile gloves for routine bathing of clients with intact skin
- **✓ C. Taking only the supplies needed for a dressing change into the client's room**

### PN-A02-023 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Delegating a task an AP has not done before

**Stem:** The LPN/VN asks an assistive personnel (AP) to collect a stool specimen for occult blood. The AP says, "I've never done that before." What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Show the AP how to collect the specimen and watch the AP do it**
- B. Tell the AP to read the procedure manual and then collect the specimen alone
- C. Assign the task to the AP anyway, since collecting specimens is simple
- D. Skip the specimen

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** The nurse delegates only to staff who are competent to do the task. When an AP has not done a task before, the nurse can teach it and observe the AP, per facility policy, or do it personally. Reading a manual does not confirm competence, assigning the task anyway risks an incorrect specimen, and skipping a prescribed test delays care.

### PN-A02-026 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care
