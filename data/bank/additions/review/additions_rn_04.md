### RN-A04-001 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: antepartum unit

**Stem:** The nurse on an antepartum unit receives report on four clients. Which client should the nurse assess first?

- **✓ A. A client at 33 weeks with placenta previa who reports a new gush of bright red, painless bleeding**
- B. A client at 28 weeks with gestational diabetes whose fasting glucose this morning was 92 mg/dL
- C. A client at 30 weeks with hyperemesis who kept down crackers and juice at breakfast
- D. A client at 36 weeks who reports Braxton Hicks contractions off and on today

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** New bright red bleeding in a client with placenta previa can quickly become a hemorrhage that threatens both the client and the fetus, so this client needs immediate assessment. A normal fasting glucose, tolerating breakfast with hyperemesis, and irregular Braxton Hicks contractions near term are stable findings.

### RN-A04-003 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Illegal substances in a client's belongings

**Stem:** While listing a newly admitted client's belongings, the nurse finds a bag of what appears to be illegal drugs. What should the nurse do?

- A. Return the bag to the client's belongings, since the client's property is private and protected by law
- **✓ B. Follow facility policy, which usually involves notifying security and securing the bag**
- C. Flush the contents down the toilet right away so that no one else on the unit can use them
- D. Let the client keep it

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Facilities have policies for suspected illegal substances, which usually call for securing the item, notifying security, and documenting objectively, without the nurse searching further or confronting the client. Returning the drugs creates a safety risk for the client and others, and destroying them is not the nurse's decision.

### RN-A04-004 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Law enforcement requests for blood samples

**Stem:** A police officer asks the nurse to draw a blood alcohol sample from a conscious, competent client involved in a car crash. The client refuses. What should the nurse do?

- A. Draw the sample, since a police officer has the authority to request it from any client
- B. Draw the sample while the client is distracted so that the client does not become upset
- **✓ C. Not draw blood without consent, a warrant, or another legal basis per policy**
- D. Ask the officer to draw the sample personally, since nurses are not allowed to be involved

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Drawing blood from a competent client who refuses, without a warrant, court order, or other legal authority, can be battery. The nurse follows facility policy, which usually involves the supervisor or risk management. A police request alone does not override the client's refusal, deceiving the client is unethical, and the officer cannot perform the procedure.

### RN-A04-007 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

### RN-A04-008 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Do-not-resuscitate orders and surgery

**Stem:** A client with a do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order is scheduled for surgery to repair a hip fracture. Which action reflects current guidelines?

- **✓ A. Make sure the team discusses the DNR order with the client before surgery**
- B. Suspend the DNR order automatically for the entire perioperative period without a discussion
- C. Cancel the surgery, since clients with DNR orders cannot undergo any procedures
- D. Keep the DNR order in place without telling the anesthesia team anything about it

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Current guidelines call for required reconsideration: a DNR order is neither automatically suspended nor ignored during surgery. The surgeon and anesthesia team discuss with the client or surrogate which interventions are acceptable during the procedure, and the plan is documented. Clients with DNR orders can have surgery, and the anesthesia team must know the client's wishes.

### RN-A04-010 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Speaking up during a surgical time-out

**Stem:** During a time-out, the nurse notices that the surgical site marked on the client does not match the consent form. The surgeon says to proceed. What should the nurse do?

- **✓ A. Restate the concern and stop the procedure until the discrepancy is resolved**
- B. Proceed as directed, since the surgeon is responsible for knowing the correct surgical site
- C. Document the discrepancy after the case
- D. Ask the anesthetized client

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Under the Universal Protocol, any team member can stop a procedure when there is a safety concern. If a first concern is dismissed, the nurse states it again clearly (the two-challenge rule) and uses the chain of command if needed. Proceeding risks wrong-site surgery, documenting afterward does not prevent harm, and an anesthetized client cannot answer.

### RN-A04-012 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Verbal prescriptions in nonemergency situations

**Stem:** A provider stops the nurse in the hallway and verbally prescribes a stool softener for a client. It is not an emergency, and the provider can use the electronic order system. What should the nurse do?

- A. Write the prescription on a paper towel and enter it into the client's record later in the shift
- B. Give the stool softener now and ask the provider to sign for it at the end of the week
- **✓ C. Ask the provider to enter the prescription in the electronic record**
- D. Ask another nurse to remember the prescription and enter it into the record at a later time

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Verbal prescriptions increase the risk of errors, so policies limit them to emergencies or situations in which the provider cannot enter them. When the provider can use the electronic system, the nurse asks the provider to enter the prescription. Notes on scraps of paper, delayed signatures, and relying on another nurse's memory invite errors.

- **✓ A. The gloves**
- B. The goggles
- C. The gown
- D. The mask

### RN-A04-023 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** N95 respirator seal check

**Stem:** Before entering the room of a client with suspected pulmonary tuberculosis, the nurse puts on a fit-tested N95 respirator. What should the nurse do next?

- A. Pinch the nosepiece loosely so the respirator is comfortable to wear during the visit
- B. Pull the bottom strap over the top of the head and the top strap under the ears
- C. Skip the seal check, since the respirator was fit-tested
- **✓ D. Perform a user seal check by exhaling and inhaling to detect leaks**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A user seal check is done every time an N95 respirator is put on to make sure air is not leaking around the edges. ### RN-A04-025 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Car seat safety

### RN-A04-026 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Side rails and bed safety

**Stem:** A confused older client keeps trying to climb out of bed. Which action by the nurse is safest?

- A. Raise all four side rails so the client cannot get out of bed without help from staff
- B. Tuck the sheets tightly
- C. Keep the room dark
- **✓ D. Lower the bed, use a bed alarm, and check on the client frequently**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A low bed, a bed alarm, frequent checks, and meeting needs such as toileting are the least restrictive ways to prevent falls. Tight sheets restrict movement, and a dark room increases confusion and fall risk.

### RN-A04-028 · Multiple choice · Safety and Infection Control

**Topic:** Relapse prevention in substance use disorder

**Stem:** A client in early recovery from opioid use disorder asks the nurse how to avoid relapsing. Which response by the nurse is most helpful?

- A. "Just avoid thinking about using, and the cravings will stop on their own soon enough."
- B. "A relapse would mean treatment has failed."
- **✓ C. "Let's identify your triggers and plan who you can call when cravings start."**
- D. "Stay home alone for now."

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Relapse prevention focuses on recognizing personal triggers, such as certain people, places, and emotions, and planning coping strategies and support, such as a sponsor, peer group, or counselor, along with continuing medication for opioid use disorder if prescribed. Trying not to think about using does not stop cravings, relapse is a common part of recovery from a chronic illness rather than proof that treatment failed, and isolation increases the risk of relapse.

### RN-A04-036 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Progressive muscle relaxation

**Stem:** The nurse is teaching progressive muscle relaxation to a client with generalized anxiety disorder. Which instruction is correct?

- A. Tense all of the muscles in the body at once and hold the tension for as long as possible
- B. Practice only during a panic attack, since the technique will not help at other times
- C. Hold the breath while tensing
- **✓ D. Tense each muscle group for a few seconds, then relax it and notice the difference**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Progressive muscle relaxation involves tensing one muscle group at a time for a few seconds and then releasing it, helping the client recognize and reduce tension. It works best when practiced regularly, not only during a crisis. Tensing everything at once and holding the breath work against relaxation.

### RN-A04-037 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Negative symptoms of schizophrenia

**Stem:** A client with schizophrenia has a flat affect, says very little, and stays in bed most of the day. Which nursing intervention is most appropriate?

- A. Let the client stay in bed, since the negative symptoms will resolve without intervention
- **✓ B. Set small, achievable daily goals for self-care**
- C. Encourage the client to lead the evening community meeting for the whole unit
- D. Confront the client about laziness so that they will feel motivated to get up

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Negative symptoms such as flat affect, alogia, and avolition respond best to structure and small, achievable goals that build success. Leaving the client in bed reinforces withdrawal, leading a large meeting is too demanding at first, and calling the client lazy is judgmental and ignores that these are symptoms of the illness.

### RN-A04-038 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Signs of elder abuse or neglect

**Stem:** The nurse is caring for an older adult brought in by a family caregiver. Which findings may indicate abuse or neglect? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Bruises in different stages of healing on the upper arms**
- **✓ B. Poor hygiene and soiled clothing**
- **✓ C. The caregiver insists on answering every question for the client**
- **✓ D. Unexplained delays in seeking care for an injury**
- E. Thin, fragile skin consistent with aging
- F. Gradual weight loss with a documented cancer diagnosis and an appropriate care plan

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** Bruises in various stages of healing, especially on the upper arms where someone might grab, poor hygiene, a caregiver who will not let the client speak, and delays in seeking care are warning signs that need further assessment and reporting. Thin skin from aging and weight loss explained by a known illness with appropriate care are not, by themselves, signs of abuse.

### RN-A04-039 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Sundowning in dementia

**Stem:** A client with Alzheimer's disease becomes agitated and confused every evening. Which interventions should the nurse include in the plan of care? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Keep a consistent daily routine**
- **✓ B. Increase lighting in the late afternoon**
- **✓ C. Reduce noise and activity in the evening**
- **✓ D. Encourage physical activity earlier in the day**
- E. Apply restraints during the evening hours
- F. Correct the client firmly each time they are confused

**Answer:** A, B, C, D

**Rationale:** A predictable routine, brighter light in the late afternoon, a calm evening environment, and daytime activity reduce sundowning. Restraints increase agitation and injury, and repeatedly correcting a confused client can increase distress; calm redirection works better.
