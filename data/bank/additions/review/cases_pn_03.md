## PN-C11: Hypoglycemia in an older adult taking a sulfonylurea

System: Endocrine · Discipline: Pharmacology

**Scenario:** The LPN/VN makes a home health visit to a 78-year-old client with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. The client's spouse called because the client "isn't making sense."

**Nurses' notes, 1100:** Sweaty, shaky, and confused. Cannot answer questions clearly but is able to swallow. Skipped breakfast because of nausea last night. Took the usual glipizide dose at 0800. The spouse says the provider increased the glipizide dose last week.

**Vital signs and glucose, 1100:** Fingerstick glucose 48 mg/dL. HR 104/min, RR 18/min, BP 142/84 mm Hg.

**Medication list and recent labs:** Glipizide 10 mg by mouth twice daily (increased from 5 mg last week). Lisinopril 10 mg by mouth daily. Creatinine 2.1 mg/dL (0.6–1.2) last month.

**Rationale:** The symptoms and a glucose of 48 mg/dL show hypoglycemia. A recent dose increase, a dose taken without food, and reduced kidney function, which slows elimination of sulfonylureas, explain it and must be reported. Being able to swallow and a normal respiratory rate are not concerns.

## PN-C12: Hepatic encephalopathy in a client with cirrhosis

System: Gastrointestinal · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** A 61-year-old client with alcohol-related cirrhosis is admitted with increasing confusion. The client stopped drinking alcohol 2 years ago.

**Nurses' notes, 0800:** Oriented to person only. Sleepy but arousable. Flapping tremor of the hands when the arms are held out. Abdomen distended with ascites. Skin and sclera yellow. One hard bowel movement in 3 days. The spouse says the client stopped taking lactulose last week because of "too many bathroom trips."

**Vital signs, 0800:** T 98.6°F (37°C), HR 88/min, RR 18/min, BP 112/70 mm Hg, SpO2 96% on room air.

**Laboratory results:** Ammonia 118 mcg/dL (15–45). Bilirubin 4.2 mg/dL (0.3–1.2). Albumin 2.6 g/dL (3.5–5.0). Platelets 82,000/mm3 (150,000–400,000).

**Prescriptions:** Lactulose 30 mL by mouth every 2 hours until 2 to 3 soft stools, then every 8 hours to keep 2 to 3 soft stools a day. Rifaximin 550 mg by mouth twice daily. Fall precautions.

### PN-C12-01 · Highlight · Physiological Adaptation · Recognize cues

**Topic:** Case study: Recognize cues

**Stem:** Highlight the findings that require follow-up.

- s5: Ammonia 118 mcg/dL

**Answer:** Oriented to person only; Flapping tremor of the hands; One hard bowel movement in 3 days; Stopped taking lactulose last week; Ammonia 118 mcg/dL

**Rationale:** Confusion, asterixis (flapping tremor), and a high ammonia level indicate hepatic encephalopathy, and constipation after stopping lactulose explains why ammonia built up. The heart rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature are normal.

## PN-C13: Infant with hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
