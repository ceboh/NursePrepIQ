## PN-C06: Acute asthma exacerbation in an adult

System: Respiratory · Discipline: Adult Health

**Scenario:** A 32-year-old client with asthma is admitted from the emergency department after 2 days of worsening wheezing that followed a cold. The client received albuterol nebulizer treatments and oral prednisone in the emergency department.

**Nurses' notes, 1400:** Sitting upright and speaking in short phrases. Expiratory wheezes in all lung fields. Using neck muscles to breathe. Says the rescue inhaler "stopped helping." Has used the rescue inhaler about 10 times a day this week. Has not been taking the daily inhaled corticosteroid because "I feel fine most of the time."

**Vital signs, 1400:** T 99.0°F (37.2°C), HR 112/min, RR 28/min, BP 128/82 mm Hg, SpO2 91% on room air.

**Prescriptions:** Albuterol nebulizer every 4 hours and as needed. Prednisone 40 mg by mouth daily. Fluticasone inhaler 2 puffs twice daily. Oxygen to keep SpO2 at 92% or higher. Peak flow before and after each nebulizer treatment.

**Answer:** Speaking in short phrases; Using neck muscles to breathe; Rescue inhaler about 10 times a day; Not taking the daily inhaled corticosteroid; RR 28/min; SpO2 91% on room air

**Rationale:** Speaking in short phrases, accessory muscle use, rapid breathing, and an oxygen saturation below the prescribed goal show significant respiratory distress. Heavy rescue inhaler use and skipping the controller medicine explain the poor control. The blood pressure and temperature are not concerns.

- Blank 2: **✓ reducing airway inflammation over time** / opening the airways within minutes / thinning mucus in the lungs

**Answer:** 1: not using the controller inhaler; 2: reducing airway inflammation over time

**Rationale:** Inhaled corticosteroids are controller medicines that reduce airway inflammation when taken every day, even when symptoms are absent. Stopping them allows inflammation to build, and the client then relies on a quick-relief inhaler that does not treat inflammation.

## PN-C07: Hip fracture repair: first postoperative day

## PN-C08: Newborn hypoglycemia after a pregnancy with gestational diabetes

System: Endocrine · Discipline: Maternal & Newborn

## PN-C10: Acute dystonia after starting an antipsychotic

**Nurses' notes, 1500:** Head twisted to the left and pulled back. Eyes rolled upward. Speech slurred, with the tongue protruding. Anxious and crying. Breathing unlabored, swallowing secretions. Received the third dose of haloperidol at 1200.
