### PN-A01-001 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: long-term care

**Stem:** The LPN/VN on a long-term care unit receives report on four residents. Which resident should the LPN/VN check on first?

- A. A resident with heart failure whose weight is unchanged from yesterday's weight
- B. A resident with diabetes who is asking what time lunch will be served on the unit
- **✓ C. A resident with new confusion, a temperature of 101°F (38.3°C), and foul-smelling urine**
- D. A resident with osteoarthritis who has knee stiffness that eases after walking

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** New confusion with fever and foul-smelling urine suggests an infection, such as a urinary tract infection that may be progressing to sepsis; older adults often show infection first as a change in mental status. The LPN/VN should collect more data and report to the RN promptly. A stable weight in heart failure, a question about mealtime, and morning stiffness that improves with activity are expected or stable findings.

### PN-A01-002 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Reporting findings to the RN

**Stem:** The LPN/VN is collecting data on a client 1 day after abdominal surgery. Which finding should the LPN/VN report to the RN immediately?

- A. Incisional pain rated 4/10 thirty minutes after an oral analgesic
- **✓ B. A respiratory rate of 28/min with new restlessness**
- C. Hypoactive bowel sounds in all four quadrants
- D. A temperature of 99.5°F (37.5°C)

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A rapid respiratory rate with new restlessness can signal hypoxemia from atelectasis, pneumonia, or a pulmonary embolism and needs immediate reporting. Moderate pain 30 minutes after an oral analgesic may still be improving and should be rechecked. Hypoactive bowel sounds are expected on the first day after abdominal surgery, and a low-grade temperature is common in the first 24 to 48 hours.

### PN-A01-003 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Delegating care for a resident with dysphagia

**Stem:** A resident with dysphagia after a stroke is on a mechanical soft diet with thickened liquids. Which task can the LPN/VN delegate to an assistive personnel (AP)?

- A. Checking whether the resident's gag reflex is present before the meal tray is served
- B. Deciding which food textures are safe for the resident at each meal
- C. Evaluating whether coughing during meals means the resident is aspirating food
- **✓ D. Seating the resident fully upright for meals and for 30 minutes afterward**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Positioning the resident upright during and after meals is a routine, standardized task that an AP can perform once instructed. Checking the gag reflex and judging whether coughing means aspiration require data collection and nursing judgment. Diet textures are prescribed after a swallowing evaluation by the speech-language pathologist and provider; they are not chosen meal by meal.

### PN-A01-004 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Following up on a finding reported by an AP

**Stem:** An assistive personnel (AP) reports that a resident's blood pressure is 86/50 mm Hg. It was 128/76 mm Hg this morning. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- **✓ A. Go to the resident, recheck the blood pressure, and collect more data**
- B. Ask the AP to recheck the resident's blood pressure in 1 hour and report the result
- C. Record the reading in the resident's chart and report it to the RN at the end of the shift
- D. Tell the AP to give the resident a large glass of water and help them lie down in bed

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** A sudden drop in blood pressure must be verified by the nurse, who also collects related data, such as heart rate, level of consciousness, and symptoms, and then reports to the RN. Waiting an hour or until the end of the shift delays care for a resident who may be unstable. Giving fluids before the cause is known may be harmful, for example in a resident with heart failure.

### PN-A01-005 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Scope of practice: admission assessment

**Stem:** The charge nurse asks the LPN/VN to complete the admission assessment for a client newly admitted with pneumonia. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Collect admission data for the RN and tell the charge nurse the RN must do the initial assessment**
- B. Complete and sign the full admission assessment, because the charge nurse made the assignment
- C. Refuse to care for the client until an RN has completed every part of the admission
- D. Ask the assistive personnel to complete the admission data while the LPN/VN gives medications

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** The initial comprehensive assessment is an RN responsibility. The LPN/VN contributes by collecting data, such as vital signs, history, and current medications, and tells the charge nurse about the scope limit. Completing the assessment alone is outside the LPN/VN's scope even when it is assigned. Refusing all care leaves the client without needed care, and collecting admission data is not an AP task.

### PN-A01-006 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Telephone prescriptions

**Stem:** Facility policy allows the LPN/VN to accept telephone prescriptions. A provider calls with a new prescription for a resident. Which action by the LPN/VN is correct?

- A. Repeat only the drug name, since the pharmacist will check the dose and route later on
- B. Ask the provider to text the prescription to a personal phone
- **✓ C. Write the prescription down and read it back to the provider to confirm it**
- D. Accept it and ask the RN to sign it

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** A telephone prescription is written down and read back in full, including the drug, dose, route, and frequency, so the provider can confirm it; the provider then signs it per policy. Repeating only the drug name skips verification of the dose and route. Texting prescriptions to a personal phone is not secure or approved, and the RN cannot sign a prescription on the provider's behalf.

### PN-A01-007 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Withdrawal of consent

**Stem:** A client who signed a consent form for a colonoscopy tells the LPN/VN, "I've changed my mind. I don't want this test." What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Remind the client that the signed form means the test has to go ahead
- **✓ B. Notify the RN and provider that the client wants to withdraw consent**
- C. Give the prescribed preprocedure sedative to help the client relax first
- D. Ask the family to talk the client into it

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A client can withdraw consent at any time, even after signing the form. The LPN/VN reports the change so that the provider can talk with the client and the procedure is not done without consent. A signed form does not obligate the client. Giving a sedative would impair the client's ability to decide and could be coercive, and pressuring the client through family does not respect autonomy.

### PN-A01-008 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Supervising a new assistive personnel

**Stem:** An assistive personnel (AP) who started on the unit this week is about to bathe a client who had a posterior hip replacement 2 days ago. What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Let the AP proceed, since bathing is a task that is part of the AP's basic training
- B. Ask the AP to watch a video about hip surgery after the bath is completed today
- C. Assign the bath to a different AP who has worked on the unit for many years
- **✓ D. Review the client's hip precautions with the AP**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** When delegating, the nurse gives clear, client-specific instructions, such as avoiding hip flexion beyond 90 degrees, crossing the legs, and turning the leg inward, and confirms that the AP understands them. Basic bathing training does not cover this client's restrictions. A video after the bath is too late, and reassigning the task does not ensure that the new AP learns to give this care safely.

### PN-A01-009 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Floating to an unfamiliar unit

**Stem:** An LPN/VN who usually works in long-term care is floated to a pediatric unit for the shift. Which response to the assignment is most appropriate?

- A. Refuse the float assignment, since working on an unfamiliar unit is always unsafe
- **✓ B. Accept tasks within their competence and tell the charge nurse which skills are unfamiliar**
- C. Accept every assigned task to avoid seeming uncooperative with the unit staff
- D. Return to the long-term care unit until the staffing office makes a change

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Nurses are accountable for accepting only tasks they are competent to perform. Telling the charge nurse which skills are unfamiliar allows the assignment to be adjusted safely. Refusing the float outright is not appropriate when the nurse can safely perform many of the tasks, accepting tasks beyond competence risks harm, and leaving the unit after accepting an assignment can be client abandonment.

### PN-A01-010 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Impaired coworker

**Stem:** At the start of the shift, the LPN/VN notices that a coworker smells of alcohol and has slurred speech. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Wait until the end of the shift and then talk privately with the coworker about it
- B. Ask the coworker to care for only the most stable clients today
- C. Cover the coworker's medication pass so the clients are safe
- **✓ D. Report the observations to the charge nurse or supervisor right away**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A nurse who appears impaired endangers clients, so the LPN/VN reports specific observations to the charge nurse or supervisor immediately, following facility policy. Waiting until the end of the shift leaves clients at risk. Limiting the coworker's assignment or covering the medication pass hides the problem and does not remove the impaired nurse from client care.

### PN-A01-011 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Documenting an unwitnessed fall

**Stem:** The LPN/VN finds a resident sitting on the floor beside the bed. The resident says, "I slipped." Which entry in the record is correct?

- A. Resident fell out of bed because the AP left the side rail down. Incident report completed.
- B. Resident fell while trying to walk to the bathroom alone without using the call light first.
- **✓ C. Found sitting on floor beside bed. States, "I slipped." No visible injury. RN notified.**
- D. Resident appears to have fallen. Seems fine.

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Documentation of an unwitnessed fall states what the nurse observed, the client's own words, the findings, and who was notified. The nurse did not see the fall, so stating its cause is an assumption; blaming staff and referring to the incident report do not belong in the record. A vague entry such as "seems fine" lacks objective data.

### PN-A01-012 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Telephone requests for client information

**Stem:** A caller says they are a client's sister and asks the LPN/VN how the client is doing after surgery. What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Check whether the client has authorized the caller to receive information**
- B. Give a brief update, since sharing information with close family is always allowed
- C. Tell the caller that no information can be given to anyone over the telephone
- D. Transfer the call to the client's room without first checking with the client

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Privacy rules let nurses share information with family members involved in care when the client agrees, so the nurse verifies authorization per facility policy, such as a privacy code or the client's stated wishes. Family members are not automatically entitled to information. A blanket refusal is not required when the client has given permission, and transferring the call without asking may reveal the client's presence to someone the client has not approved.

### PN-A01-013 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Security of electronic health information

**Stem:** Which action by the LPN/VN protects the security of electronic health information?

- A. Sharing a password with a float nurse who is waiting for access
- **✓ B. Logging off the workstation every time before stepping away from it**
- C. Saving a client list to a personal flash drive to review at home
- D. Leaving a record open on the screen for the next nurse to use

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Logging off prevents others from viewing records or documenting under the nurse's credentials. Passwords are never shared; the float nurse should obtain access through the proper process. Client information is not stored on personal devices, and a record left open on the screen can be seen by anyone passing by.

### PN-A01-014 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Quality improvement

**Stem:** The LPN/VN notices that several residents on the unit have fallen during the evening shift change over the past month. Which action best supports quality improvement?

- A. Assume that the falls are random events and keep providing care as usual on the unit
- B. Restrain residents at risk of falling during shift change
- **✓ C. Share the pattern with the nurse manager so the unit can look for causes**
- D. Keep the pattern private to protect the evening staff

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Quality improvement depends on staff reporting patterns so the team can analyze causes, such as gaps in rounding or call-light response during shift change, and test changes. Treating a pattern as random prevents improvement. Restraints do not prevent falls and can increase injury, and keeping the pattern private protects no one.

### PN-A01-015 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Referral for medication costs

**Stem:** A client who is going home tomorrow tells the LPN/VN, "I don't know how I'll pay for all these new medicines." What should the LPN/VN do?

- **✓ A. Tell the RN so that a referral to social work or case management can be made**
- B. Suggest that the client take the medicines every other day so that they last longer
- C. Advise the client to stop the most expensive medicine until it becomes affordable
- D. Reassure the client that the cost will work out

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Social workers and case managers can help with prescription assistance programs, lower-cost alternatives, and insurance coverage; the LPN/VN reports the concern to the RN, who coordinates the referral. Changing the dosing schedule or stopping a medication without the provider can be dangerous, and false reassurance leaves the problem unsolved.

### PN-A01-016 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Transfer report

**Stem:** A client is being transferred from the hospital to a skilled nursing facility. Which information is most important for the LPN/VN to include in the report to the receiving nurse?

- A. Details of the client's insurance coverage and the billing contact
- B. The names of all of the client's visitors during the hospital stay
- C. The client's choices from the hospital menu on each day of the stay
- **✓ D. Current medications and the time each was last given**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Knowing the current medications and when each was last given prevents missed or duplicated doses during the transition, which is a high-risk time for errors. Insurance details are handled by the business office, and visitor names and past menu choices do not affect safe care.

### PN-A01-017 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Health care power of attorney

**Stem:** A client with advanced dementia can no longer make decisions, and a decision is needed about placing a feeding tube. The client's durable power of attorney for health care names their son. Who should make the decision?

- A. The client's spouse, since a spouse's choice overrides the named agent
- **✓ B. The son, as the named agent, based on the client's known wishes and values**
- C. The provider, since the client can no longer make choices
- D. The facility's ethics committee, by majority vote

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A health care agent named in a durable power of attorney for health care makes decisions when the client cannot, based on the client's known wishes and values. The named agent's authority takes priority over relatives who would otherwise decide by default. The provider advises but does not decide for the client, and an ethics committee consults on disputes rather than making routine decisions.

### PN-A01-018 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Using the chain of command

**Stem:** The LPN/VN reports to the RN that a client's blood pressure has fallen from 132/80 to 92/58 mm Hg over 2 hours. The RN says to recheck it in 4 hours. The client now reports dizziness. What should the LPN/VN do next?

- **✓ A. Report the client's status and the RN's response to the charge nurse or supervisor**
- B. Recheck the blood pressure in 4 hours as directed, since the RN is responsible for the client's care
- C. Call the client's family and ask them to come in
- D. Give the client an extra liter of IV fluid

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** When a client's condition is worsening and the nurse's concern is not addressed, the nurse uses the chain of command to advocate for the client. Following a plan the LPN/VN believes is unsafe does not remove the LPN/VN's own accountability. Calling the family does not get the client evaluated, and giving IV fluid without a prescription is outside the nurse's authority.

### PN-A01-019 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Conflict with an assistive personnel

**Stem:** An assistive personnel (AP) refuses when the LPN/VN asks for help repositioning a client, saying, "That's not my assignment." What should the LPN/VN do first?

- A. Report the AP to the director of nursing and request a written warning for refusing
- B. Discuss the refusal at the next team huddle in front of the other staff on the unit
- C. Reposition the client without help
- **✓ D. Talk with the AP privately to understand the concern and clarify expectations**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A conflict is best addressed first through a private, direct conversation to understand the other person's view and clarify expectations; the AP may be overloaded or unclear about the assignment. Going to the director before talking with the AP skips that step, and raising it in front of others can create defensiveness. Repositioning alone can injure the nurse or the client and leaves the conflict unresolved.

### PN-A01-020 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Working with a medical interpreter

**Stem:** The LPN/VN is reinforcing discharge teaching with a client who speaks Vietnamese, using a trained medical interpreter. Which action by the LPN/VN is appropriate?

- A. Speak to the interpreter and ask the interpreter to tell the client what was said
- B. Ask the interpreter to summarize the teaching in their own words to save time
- **✓ C. Speak directly to the client in short sentences**

### PN-A01-021 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: home health visits

**Stem:** The LPN/VN working in home health has four visits scheduled today. Which client should the LPN/VN visit first?

- **✓ A. A client with heart failure who reports gaining 3 lb (1.4 kg) since yesterday and new ankle swelling**
- B. A client with a stable venous leg ulcer who needs a scheduled dressing change this afternoon
- C. A client with type 2 diabetes who needs reinforcement of foot care teaching
- D. A client 3 weeks after a hip replacement who walks well with a walker

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** A weight gain of 2 to 3 lb (about 1 kg) or more in a day with new edema suggests fluid retention from worsening heart failure, which needs prompt data collection and reporting to the RN or provider. A scheduled dressing change, foot care teaching, and a client recovering as expected after hip replacement can be seen later.

### PN-A01-022 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Information about hospice

**Stem:** A client with end-stage COPD asks the LPN/VN what hospice care means. Which response by the LPN/VN is accurate?

- A. "Hospice care is only provided in a special facility during the last few days of life."
- **✓ B. "Hospice focuses on comfort and quality of life when the goal is to relieve symptoms rather than cure."**
- C. "Hospice means you will not get any medications, even those for pain."
- D. "Once you choose hospice, you can never go back to treatment aimed at a cure, even if you change your mind."

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Hospice provides comfort-focused care, including symptom management and support for the family, for clients with a limited life expectancy who are no longer seeking curative treatment. It is usually provided at home but can also be given in facilities, and it is not limited to the last few days of life. Medications that relieve symptoms, including pain medicine, continue. A client can stop hospice and return to curative treatment at any time.

### PN-A01-023 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Negligence

**Stem:** Which situation is an example of negligence by the LPN/VN?

- A. Documenting a client's refusal of a medication and notifying the RN of the refusal
- B. Keeping a competent client in their room by locking the door because they ask many questions
- C. Giving a prescribed medication after checking two client identifiers
- **✓ D. Not reporting a client's falling blood pressure, which delays treatment**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Negligence is a failure to meet the standard of care that causes harm; failing to report a significant change that delays treatment fits this definition. Documenting and reporting a refusal and checking two identifiers meet the standard of care. Locking a competent client in a room is false imprisonment, an intentional tort rather than negligence.

### PN-A01-024 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Collaborating with the dietitian

**Stem:** A resident in long-term care has eaten less than half of each meal for a week and has lost 4 lb (1.8 kg). Which team member should the LPN/VN recommend that the RN involve?

- A. The respiratory therapist
- B. The physical therapist
- **✓ C. The dietitian**

**Answer:** C

The respiratory therapist manages breathing treatments, the physical therapist addresses mobility, and a pharmacy technician prepares medications; none of them addresses poor intake and weight loss directly.

### PN-A01-025 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Questioning an assignment

**Stem:** The LPN/VN is assigned a client who was transferred from the ICU an hour ago and whose oxygen needs are increasing. What should the LPN/VN do?

- A. Accept the client and avoid calling the RN unless the oxygen saturation falls below 80%
- B. Trade clients with another LPN/VN without telling the charge nurse about it

### PN-A01-026 · Multiple choice · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Event reports and the medical record

**Stem:** A client on a low-sodium diet was served a regular tray and ate part of it. The LPN/VN completes an event (incident) report. Which action is correct?

- A. Write in the progress notes that an event report was filed so the other staff are aware
- B. Place a copy of the event report in the client's medical record

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Event reports are internal quality documents. The nurse documents the facts and the client's condition in the record but does not mention or copy the report, which could make it part of the legal record. Clients do not sign event reports.

### PN-A01-027 · Select all that apply · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Rights of residents in long-term care

**Stem:** Which actions by the LPN/VN respect the rights of residents in a long-term care facility? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Knocking and waiting for a response before entering a resident's room**
- **✓ B. Letting a resident choose which clothes to wear each day**
- C. Opening a resident's mail to check for upsetting news
- **✓ D. Supporting a resident's decision to skip a group activity**
- E. Requiring every resident to be in bed by 9:00 p.m.
- **✓ F. Keeping a resident's family photos and personal items in the room**

**Answer:** A, B, D, F

**Rationale:** Federal regulations protect residents' rights to privacy, dignity, self-determination, and personal possessions. Knocking before entering, choosing clothing, declining activities, and keeping personal belongings support those rights. Residents have the right to private, unopened mail, and a facility-wide bedtime ignores individual choice.

### PN-A01-028 · Select all that apply · Coordinated Care

### PN-A01-032 · Select all that apply · Coordinated Care

**Topic:** Referral to home health nursing

The client received IV morphine for back pain 20 minutes ago, is drowsy, and asks, "What test am I having today?" Respiratory rate is 12/min.
