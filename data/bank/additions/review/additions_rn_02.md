### RN-A02-001 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: rehabilitation unit

**Stem:** The nurse on an inpatient rehabilitation unit receives report on four clients. Which client should the nurse assess first?

- A. A client 10 days after a stroke who becomes frustrated and cries during speech therapy
- **✓ B. A client with a T4 spinal cord injury who has a pounding headache and a BP of 188/104 mm Hg**
- C. A client with a below-knee amputation who reports cramping pain in the missing foot
- D. A client with a traumatic brain injury who needs reminders to use the call light

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** A pounding headache and severe hypertension in a client with a spinal cord injury at T6 or above suggest autonomic dysreflexia, an emergency that can cause a stroke or seizure; the nurse sits the client up and looks for the trigger, such as a full bladder. Emotional lability after a stroke, phantom limb pain, and memory deficits after a brain injury need attention but are not immediately life-threatening.

### RN-A02-002 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Prioritization: ambulatory surgery center

**Stem:** The nurse in an ambulatory surgery center is caring for four clients after their procedures. Which client should the nurse see first?

- A. A client after cataract surgery who reports mild scratchiness in the operative eye
- B. A client after a colonoscopy who has bloating and is passing gas
- **✓ C. A client 1 hour after a tonsillectomy who is swallowing repeatedly**
- D. A client after a breast biopsy who rates pain 3/10

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** Frequent swallowing after a tonsillectomy suggests bleeding from the surgical site, which can threaten the airway and cause significant blood loss. Mild scratchiness after cataract surgery, bloating from insufflated air after a colonoscopy, and mild pain after a biopsy are expected findings.

### RN-A02-003 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Scope of a do-not-resuscitate order

**Stem:** A client with a do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order is found diaphoretic and confused, with a blood glucose of 48 mg/dL. What should the nurse do?

- A. Notify the family before giving treatment, since the DNR order limits all medical care
- B. Keep the client comfortable without any treatment, as the DNR order requires
- C. Ask the provider to change the DNR order first
- **✓ D. Treat the hypoglycemia per protocol, since a DNR order applies only to CPR**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** A DNR order means that CPR will not be started if the client's heart or breathing stops. It does not limit other treatment, so the nurse treats the hypoglycemia right away per protocol. Delaying care to call the family or provider, or withholding treatment, misreads the order and leaves a reversible problem untreated.

### RN-A02-004 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Duty to warn

**Stem:** During an outpatient mental health visit, a client describes a specific plan to harm a former coworker. What is the nurse's legal responsibility?

- **✓ A. Report the threat to the treatment team so the person can be warned**
- B. Keep the statement confidential, since it was made during a counseling session
- C. Wait to see whether the client repeats the threat at the next scheduled visit
- D. Tell the client that nurses cannot be told about plans to harm other people

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** When a client makes a credible threat against an identifiable person, the duty to warn and protect overrides confidentiality. The nurse reports the threat to the provider and treatment team, who follow state law and policy to warn the person or notify law enforcement. Keeping it confidential or waiting puts the person at risk, and discouraging the client from sharing such plans removes the chance to intervene.

### RN-A02-005 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Assignment despite objection

**Stem:** Because of a staffing shortage, the charge nurse assigns a nurse two more clients than usual. The nurse believes the assignment is unsafe. What should the nurse do?

- A. Refuse the assignment and go home, since an unsafe assignment cannot be accepted
- B. Accept the assignment without comment to avoid conflict with the charge nurse
- C. Post about the unsafe staffing on social media to raise public awareness
- **✓ D. Accept the assignment, state the safety concerns, and complete the facility's objection form**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** When an assignment seems unsafe, the nurse states the concerns to the charge nurse or supervisor and documents them through the facility's assignment-despite-objection or similar process, while giving the best care possible and prioritizing. Leaving can be abandonment of clients who need care, staying silent prevents the problem from being addressed, and posting on social media can breach confidentiality and policy without fixing the staffing.

### RN-A02-006 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Root cause analysis

**Stem:** After a sentinel event in which a client received a tenfold overdose of insulin, the facility conducts a root cause analysis. What is the main purpose of this analysis?

- A. To identify which nurse made the error so that the nurse can be disciplined fairly
- B. To determine how much money the facility owes the client
- **✓ C. To find the system and process factors that allowed the error to happen**
- D. To decide whether the client should be told

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** A root cause analysis looks beyond individual blame to the systems and processes, such as look-alike products, missing double checks, or order-entry design, that allowed the error, so they can be redesigned. Its purpose is not discipline or deciding on compensation. Clients are told about errors that reach them regardless of the analysis.

### RN-A02-007 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Precepting a new nurse

**Stem:** The nurse is precepting a newly licensed nurse. Which action by the new nurse requires the preceptor to intervene?

- A. Asking the preceptor to watch the new nurse's first nasogastric tube insertion
- **✓ B. Preparing to give potassium chloride by IV push for a low potassium level**
- C. Using teach-back after reinforcing instructions about a new inhaler with the client
- D. Checking two identifiers before hanging an antibiotic

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Potassium chloride is never given by IV push because a rapid dose can cause fatal dysrhythmias; it must be diluted and given by infusion pump at a controlled rate. Asking for supervision with a new skill, using teach-back, and checking two identifiers are safe practices the preceptor should reinforce.

### RN-A02-008 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Collaborating with the pharmacist

**Stem:** An 84-year-old client who takes 14 prescribed medications has fallen twice this month. Which collaboration is most likely to reduce the risk of falls related to medications?

- **✓ A. The pharmacist**
- B. The physical therapist
- C. The occupational therapist
- D. The registered dietitian

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** A pharmacist can review the medication list for drugs that increase fall risk in older adults, such as sedatives, anticholinergics, and antihypertensives, and recommend changes to the provider. Physical and occupational therapists address strength, gait, and the home environment, which also help prevent falls but do not address the medications. A dietitian focuses on nutrition.

### RN-A02-009 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Case management and readmissions

**Stem:** A client with COPD has been readmitted three times in 2 months. Which action by the case manager is most likely to reduce future readmissions?

- A. Telling the client that another readmission will not be covered by insurance
- **✓ B. Coordinating home oxygen, a follow-up visit within 7 days, and pulmonary rehabilitation**
- C. Arranging for the client to stay in the hospital longer during each admission
- D. Giving the client a printed list of COPD facts at the time of discharge

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Coordinated transitional care, including needed equipment, early follow-up, and pulmonary rehabilitation, addresses the causes of repeated readmissions. Threats about coverage do not solve the client's problems, longer stays do not prevent problems after discharge, and printed facts alone do not provide the support the client needs.

### RN-A02-010 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Copying documentation forward

**Stem:** A nurse copies the previous shift's respiratory assessment into the current note because the client "has not changed." How should this practice be described?

- A. Acceptable, as long as the client's condition was stable during the previous shift
- B. Acceptable, because it saves time for client care
- C. Unsafe, because only the provider may copy notes
- **✓ D. Unsafe, because the entry may not reflect the client's current condition**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Copying an earlier assessment forward can carry outdated or inaccurate findings into the record and suggests an assessment was done when it was not. Each entry must reflect the nurse's own current assessment. Saving time does not justify inaccurate documentation, and the problem is accuracy, not who is allowed to copy notes.

### RN-A02-012 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Scope of practice: provider procedures

**Stem:** A provider asks the nurse to insert a chest tube while the provider attends to another emergency. What should the nurse do?

- **✓ A. Decline, since the procedure is outside the RN scope of practice**
- B. Insert the chest tube, since the provider has given a direct verbal order to do it
- C. Insert the chest tube if another nurse is available to assist with the procedure
- D. Ask the respiratory therapist to insert the chest tube instead of the provider

**Answer:** A

**Rationale:** Chest tube insertion is a provider procedure. A verbal order does not expand the nurse's scope of practice, and having help does not make it legal. The nurse can prepare the equipment and client and seek another qualified provider, such as through the rapid response team, if the need is urgent. Respiratory therapists do not insert chest tubes.

### RN-A02-013 · Multiple choice · Management of Care

**Topic:** Second opinions

**Stem:** A client scheduled for back surgery asks the nurse whether it is all right to get a second opinion first. What is the nurse's best response?

- **✓ A. "You have the right to a second opinion. I can let your surgeon know you'd like one."**
- B. "Getting a second opinion might offend your surgeon, so it's best to avoid it."
- C. "Your insurance will not pay for surgery if you ask for a second opinion."

### RN-A02-016 · Select all that apply · Management of Care

### RN-A02-021 · Highlight · Management of Care

**Topic:** Decision-making capacity

**Stem:** The nurse is present while the surgeon discusses a below-knee amputation with a client who has gangrene of the foot. Highlight the findings that indicate the client may lack the capacity to make this decision.

Key (highlight): Says the foot is "just a bruise" when shown the blackened toes; Cannot recall the surgeon's explanation 5 minutes later; States the surgeon wants the leg "to sell it."

- s1: Says the foot is "just a bruise" when shown the blackened toes.
- s2: Cannot recall the surgeon's explanation 5 minutes later.
- s3: States the surgeon wants the leg "to sell it."
- s4: Answers questions about their name and the date correctly.
- s5: Asks how long recovery will take.
- s6: Has a sister present for support.

**Answer:** Says the foot is "just a bruise" when shown the blackened toes; Cannot recall the surgeon's explanation 5 minutes later; States the surgeon wants the leg "to sell it."

**Rationale:** Decision-making capacity requires that the client understand the information, appreciate how it applies to their own situation, reason about the options, and communicate a choice. Denying obvious gangrene, being unable to retain the explanation, and reasoning from a false belief suggest the client may lack capacity, so the nurse reports these findings to the surgeon. Being oriented does not by itself show capacity, and asking about recovery and having family present do not suggest impairment.

### RN-A02-023 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Prolonged grief

**Stem:** Which statement by a client whose spouse died 2 years ago suggests prolonged (complicated) grief?

- A. "I feel sad on our wedding anniversary, but I've started volunteering at the library, and I enjoy it."
- **✓ B. "I still can't accept the death, and I think about my spouse all day, every day."**
- C. "Sometimes I talk to my spouse's photo, and it comforts me."
- D. "I gave away most of the clothes but kept a sweater."

**Answer:** B

**Rationale:** Prolonged grief disorder involves intense yearning for or preoccupation with the person who died nearly every day, often with disbelief or inability to accept the death, lasting at least 12 months in adults and causing distress or impairment. Sadness on anniversaries while building new activities, comfort from continuing bonds, and keeping a meaningful item are part of normal grieving.

### RN-A02-024 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Crisis intervention

**Stem:** A client is brought to the emergency department after their house burned down. The client is pacing and crying and says, "I don't know what to do." What is the nurse's priority?

- A. Explore how the client's childhood experiences affect the client's coping now
- B. Give the client a list of community shelters and ask them to call each one
- **✓ C. Make sure the client is physically safe and stay with the client**
- D. Have the client describe the fire in detail

**Answer:** C

**Rationale:** In a crisis, the first priority is the client's physical safety, followed by support and help with immediate problems. A client in acute crisis may not be able to act alone, so the nurse stays and takes a more directive role. Exploring past experiences is not appropriate during a crisis, and pushing the client to relive the event in detail can increase distress.

### RN-A02-025 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Severe anxiety

**Stem:** A client with severe anxiety is pacing, has trouble focusing, and cannot follow instructions. Which nursing action is most appropriate?

- A. Teach the client relaxation breathing techniques in a group class
- B. Ask the client to explain in detail what is causing the anxiety
- C. Leave the client alone in a busy area to calm down
- **✓ D. Use short, simple sentences in a calm voice**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** In severe anxiety, the perceptual field is greatly narrowed and learning is not possible, so the nurse stays calm and uses short, simple statements. ### RN-A02-026 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Chest pain in a client with panic disorder

**Stem:** A client in the clinic suddenly reports chest tightness, a pounding heart, and a fear of dying. The client has a history of panic disorder. What should the nurse do first?

- A. Tell the client this is a panic attack and that it will pass in a few minutes
- B. Teach the client to identify thoughts that trigger the panic attacks
- C. Leave the client alone in a quiet room until the symptoms subside
- **✓ D. Check vital signs and assess for a physical cause before attributing it to panic**

**Answer:** D

**Rationale:** Chest tightness and palpitations can come from a cardiac or other physical cause, even in a client with panic disorder, so the nurse first assesses vital signs and follows the chest pain protocol. Labeling the episode as panic without assessment could miss a myocardial infarction. Cognitive techniques are taught when the client is calm, and a client with chest pain or panic should not be left alone.

### RN-A02-031 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Rituals in obsessive-compulsive disorder

**Stem:** A newly admitted client with obsessive-compulsive disorder washes their hands for 30 minutes before every meal and is often late to eat. What should the nurse do initially?

- A. Lock the bathroom so the client cannot wash, and explain that the rule is for their own good

- **✓ C. Allow time for the ritual and plan the schedule so the client can still eat**

### RN-A02-032 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Validation in dementia care

**Stem:** A client with moderate Alzheimer's disease repeatedly asks, "Where is my mother? I need to go home to her." The client's mother died many years ago. What is the best response?

- **✓ A. "You're thinking about your mother. Tell me about her."**
- B. "Your mother died many years ago. You live here with us now."
- C. "Your mother is on her way. She'll be here after lunch today."

**Answer:** A

Inventing a visit misleads the client and increases agitation when the mother does not arrive, and a dismissive response ignores the client's distress.

### RN-A02-033 · Multiple choice · Psychosocial Integrity

### RN-A02-034 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Acute mania

**Stem:** Which interventions are appropriate for a client in an acute manic episode? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Provide high-calorie finger foods the client can eat while moving**
- **✓ B. Reduce stimulation, such as noise and group activities**
- **✓ C. Set clear, consistent limits on intrusive behavior**
- D. Encourage the client to lead a competitive group game
- E. Allow the client to stay up late to use up extra energy
- **✓ F. Give brief, simple explanations**

**Answer:** A, B, C, F

**Rationale:** A client in acute mania is easily overstimulated and too active to sit for meals, so care includes a low-stimulation environment, portable high-calorie foods, consistent limits, and brief explanations. Competitive games increase agitation, and staying up late worsens the sleep loss that fuels mania; the nurse promotes rest instead.

### RN-A02-035 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Anorexia nervosa

**Stem:** Which interventions should the nurse include in the care of a client hospitalized with anorexia nervosa? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Observe the client during meals and for a set time afterward**
- **✓ B. Weigh the client at the same time each day, in a hospital gown, after voiding**
- **✓ C. Focus conversations on the client's feelings rather than on food**
- D. Allow the client to exercise in the room after meals to manage anxiety
- E. Let the client skip meals when not hungry
- F. Bargain about weight goals each day

**Answer:** A, B, C

**Rationale:** Supervising meals and the period afterward prevents hiding food, purging, and excessive exercise; consistent weighing gives accurate data; and focusing on feelings addresses the underlying issues rather than reinforcing the focus on food. Exercise after meals burns needed calories, skipping meals delays nutritional recovery, and daily bargaining undermines the treatment plan.

### RN-A02-036 · Select all that apply · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Supporting parents after a stillbirth

**Stem:** A client has delivered a stillborn infant at 36 weeks. Which nursing actions support the family's grieving? Select all that apply.

- **✓ A. Offer the parents the chance to see and hold the infant**
- **✓ B. Refer to the infant by name if the parents have chosen one**
- **✓ C. Offer mementos, such as footprints, a lock of hair, or photographs**
- D. Tell the parents they can have another baby soon
- E. Remove all signs of the pregnancy from the room without asking
- **✓ F. Allow as much time as the parents need with the infant**

**Answer:** A, B, C, F

**Rationale:** Seeing and holding the infant, using the infant's name, keeping mementos, and unhurried time help parents acknowledge their loss and begin grieving. Mentioning a future pregnancy minimizes the loss of this baby, and removing reminders without asking takes choices away from the parents.

### RN-A02-039 · Drop-down cloze · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Stages of change

**Stem:** Complete the sentences by choosing from the lists of options.

Template: A client who says, "I know my drinking is a problem, and I'm thinking about cutting back in the next few months," is in the ___1___ stage of change. The most helpful nursing approach is to ___2___.

- Blank 1: **✓ contemplation** / precontemplation / action
- Blank 2: **✓ explore the pros and cons of changing** / insist on an immediate plan to stop drinking / wait until the client asks for help

**Answer:** 1: contemplation; 2: explore the pros and cons of changing

**Rationale:** In contemplation, a person recognizes the problem and is considering change within about 6 months but is ambivalent. Exploring the pros and cons, as in motivational interviewing, helps resolve that ambivalence. Precontemplation means the person does not yet see a problem, and action means the person has already started changing. Demanding an immediate plan can increase resistance, and waiting misses the chance to build motivation.

### RN-A02-040 · Highlight · Psychosocial Integrity

**Topic:** Escalating aggression

**Stem:** The nurse reviews the following notes about a client on an inpatient psychiatric unit. Highlight the findings that indicate the client's behavior is escalating toward aggression.

Key (highlight): Pacing rapidly in the hallway for the past 20 minutes; Clenching fists and jaw while talking with staff; Speaking loudly and swearing at a peer; Staring intently at a staff member who set a limit

- s1: Pacing rapidly in the hallway for the past 20 minutes.
- s2: Ate all of breakfast and lunch.
- s3: Clenching fists and jaw while talking with staff.
- s4: Attended the morning group and took part.
- s5: Speaking loudly and swearing at a peer.
- s6: Took scheduled medications without difficulty.
- s7: Staring intently at a staff member who set a limit.

**Answer:** Pacing rapidly in the hallway for the past 20 minutes; Clenching fists and jaw while talking with staff; Speaking loudly and swearing at a peer; Staring intently at a staff member who set a limit

**Rationale:** Rapid pacing, clenched fists and jaw, a loud voice with swearing, and intense staring are early warning signs of escalating aggression; the nurse should begin de-escalation, such as speaking calmly, offering choices, and reducing stimulation. Eating meals, attending group, and taking medications are signs of cooperation.
